[h1]Traditional Preservation (Fish & Meat)[/h1]

Preserve your catch and your kill the old way, with salt, wood, fat, smoke and the weather. One workshop for both: the same Salting Tub cures fish and meat, the same drying stations hang both, a Smokehouse smokes both, and butchering gives the fat that seals meat away in a clay jar. It all keeps for weeks, months or even years, with no fridge and no power.

[b]Build 42 only (42.22 or newer). This is an early release, so back up your save before enabling it. Singleplayer only: multiplayer is not supported.[/b]

[b]Requires Traditional Preservation (Core)[/b]: subscribe to it too, and enable it in the Mods menu (it loads first).

[h2]Salting (fish and meat)[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]Salting Tub:[/b] Carpentry 2. 4 planks, 6 nails, 2 twine, hammer and saw kept. Holds 50 weight or 35 pieces, fish or meat, never both at once.
[*][b]Pack Fish in Salt:[/b] a raw fish or fillet and a sharp knife or meat cleaver (kept). 2 salt for each quarter weight, about 15%, as on the Volga: a small panfish takes 2, a 4.5 weight walleye 32. A fish over 4.5 weight is too thick to salt whole: gut and split it first.
[*][b]Pack Meat in Salt:[/b] a Beef, Steak, Mutton Chop, Venison, Pork or Pork Chop as the butcher gives it, with 4 salt, or a whole Leg of Pork with 10. No knife needed. Pork never hangs in the open: its fat goes rancid in sun and wind.
[*][b]Leg of Pork:[/b] a grown pig gives some of its Pork as whole legs for country ham, one or two on a Butchering Hook. Five Pork make a leg, so the pig's meat stays the same.
[*][b]Cure Salted Fish / Corn Salted Meat:[/b] the heaviest piece sets the time, 1 to 2 in-game days: Salt Cured fish, Corned meat. Nothing else goes in until the last piece is out.
[*][b]Country Cure Salted Meat:[/b] the long cure for the Smokehouse's dry cured meat and country ham: 10 days for cuts, 21 with a leg. A cold-season job: safe while the last ten days average 10 C or less, about October to April. Warmer, it spoils by the bone; at 15 C a leg rots in the tub.
[*][b]Rinse Off Salt (optional):[/b] 0.15 L of clean water a piece, or at a sink. Less salty, keeps a tenth less long.
[/list]

[h2]Drying stations[/h2]
Each press of Craft hangs one stack, which keeps its spot until collected. Hover a stack to see its method, what it will come out as, drying rate, smoke and any spoilage. Weights are inventory weight, not kilograms.
[list]
[*][b]Drying Frame:[/b] Carpentry 2. 6 long sticks, 3 nails, 4 twine, hammer kept. 8 stacks of 2.5 weight. It can stand anywhere, a room or a basement too.
[*][b]Hanging Beam:[/b] Carpentry 4. 6 planks, 6 nails, 1 large hook, hammer kept. 4 stacks of 15. Anywhere, a room or a basement too.
[*][b]Gantry:[/b] Carpentry 6. 2 logs, 2 planks, 8 nails, 2 large hooks, hammer kept. 2 stacks of 50. Open air or under an open roof.
[*][b]Smokehouse[/b] (TP Core's): Carpentry 3. 14 planks, 20 nails, hammer kept. 6 stacks of 15. Two tiles; open air or under an open roof. Salt fish, meat and sausage.
[/list]

[h2]Salt Dried fish[/h2]
Hang Salt Cured Fish to Dry: 12 days at best, 20 C or warmer. Cool weather slows it, frost doesn't stop it. Salt Dried fish keeps 4 to 6 months, is very salty but safe, and goes straight into soup, stew, rice or pasta. Soak Fish (1 L of water) for a mild fish that cooks like fresh.

[h2]Charque[/h2]
Hang Salted Meat to Dry (beef, mutton, venison): 5 days in hot sun (25 C and up) and a breeze, about a week in a Kentucky summer under a roof. It slows in cooler weather and stops at freezing; rain on an open station takes back a tenth every 6 hours. Charque keeps 4 to 6 months at about half the raw weight. Beef Charque keeps 4 to 6 months; Soak Charque (1 L of water) before eating, then cook it or put it in a stew.

[h2]Stockfish[/h2]
Gut Fish (a big one also gives a Fish Head), split it if it's over 2.5 weight, and Hang Gutted Fish to Cold Dry in the open or under an open roof. No salt, no tub. 32 days at best between 0 and 5 C in a good wind; about 50 to 55 days hung November to January. It rots once the season warms up. Stockfish keeps 1 to 2 years and weighs almost nothing, but it's as hard as wood: Soak Fish before eating. It matures over 60 days in storage, faster on a Stockfish Pallet in a loft, and matured stockfish cheers you up. Tie it into a Stockfish Bale to carry it.

[h2]The Smokehouse: hot and cold smoking[/h2]
[list]
[*]Inside it's always [b]In a shed[/b]: no rain, no wind, the day's temperature (the power grid doesn't warm it), about two thirds of the open drying speed. No stockfish: gutted fish need moving air. The tile in front of its door must stay clear.
[*][b]Connect the Stone Hearth[/b] (from TP Core) on a tile beside it: a clay pipe (2 Clay Pipe Segments, 1 Clay, mason's trowel kept) or a stone trench (3 Flat Stones, 2 Clay, shovel kept). Not at the door end, not with the Stone Hearth's door towards the shed, one Stone Hearth a shed, and only with the fire out. Disconnect gives the pipe or stones back.
[*][b]Hot smoking:[/b] keep the joined Stone Hearth stoked and each salt stack inside counts the hours in a row: 4 to 6 for fish, 6 to 8 for meat, by the heaviest piece. Then it comes out Hot Smoked: cooked, ready to eat, 1 to 2 weeks' keeping. Banking the fire for up to an hour doesn't break the run. Pork comes out as a Ham you can slice. Stop sooner and fish dries on as Salt Dried; corned meat must get its cook, or it spoils in the shed.
[*][b]Hot smoked poultry and rabbit:[/b] chicken and turkey cuts, whole birds and rabbit take a light brine (Corn Salted Meat; a whole chicken 38 hours, a turkey 48) and then a hot cook in the Smokehouse, nothing else: no charque, dry curing or cold smoke, as on the real farm. A stack only waits for its stoked hours (a cut about 6, a whole bird 7 to 8) and spoils until it gets them. Carve Chicken (Hot Smoked) and Carve Turkey (Hot Smoked) cut a smoked bird into legs, wings and fillets.
[*][b]Cold smoking:[/b] bank the Stone Hearth to a smoulder. Banked hours, and stoked runs that broke off, count as smoke on days of 15 C or less. With its hours of smoke (a day or two for fish, 60 hours for cuts, 120 for a leg, by the heaviest piece) a stack comes out Cold Smoked fish, or smoked dry cured meat named by its animal: Beef, Mutton, Venison or Pork (Cold Smoked), and Smoked Country Ham from a leg. Dry, ready to eat sliced thin, 4 to 6 months' keeping. A burning fire also warms the shed, so it dries faster even in frost.
[*][b]Dry curing:[/b] meat hung in the Smokehouse is never charque. Country cured, it dries slowly in cool air: 3 weeks at best for a cut, 6 for a leg, longer in frost. Without smoke it comes out air dried: Beef, Mutton, Venison or Pork (Dry Cured), and a Country Ham from a leg, which keeps half a year to a year. No Smokehouse yet? Country cured meat dries the same way on any drying station under a roof, on a Frame or Beam indoors, and in a basement by the cellar's own temperature: about a month for a cut from June to October, two to three months in midwinter, twice that for a leg.
[*][b]Ageing:[/b] dry cured meat ages after it has dried: 30 days for a cut, 90 for a Country Ham, or three quarters of that if you leave it hanging where it dried (once taken down it can't go back up). Aged meat lifts the mood more, and an aged Country Ham takes the boredom off too.
[*][b]Slices:[/b] slice a dry cured or hot smoked cut (2 to 6 slices by its size), or carve a Country Ham 5 slices at a time, 15 from a leg, and keep the rest on the bone. Slices go wherever Ham Slices go (sandwiches, pizza, salads, the pot) and keep half as long as the whole piece.
[*]A smoked ham hangs by the door while finished stacks wait inside.
[/list]

[h2]Lard, Tallow and Cracklings[/h2]
Put Pork Fat (or Suet) in a Pot, cook it an hour on any fire, Pour Off the melted fat, and Strain it through a cloth: a block of Lard or Tallow and a portion of Cracklings for every two pieces. Don't leave it on the heat too long. A Stone Hearth's Forged Cauldron melts a whole pig's fat at once and never burns it.

[h2]Potted Meat[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]Put Meat in Lard or Tallow:[/b] an empty pot, a block of Lard or Tallow, a cut and 1 salt. Or [b]Put Meat in the Melted Fat[/b] straight from rendering.
[*][b]Add Meat to the Pot:[/b] up to four cuts. Beef, Steak, Mutton Chop, Venison, Pork and Pork Chop, or chicken, turkey and rabbit, in any mix.
[*]Cook it two hours on any fire. It can burn.
[*][b]Pack Potted Meat[/b] into a Clay Jar, Glazed Clay Jar or Clay Pot: a Crock of Potted Meat, one portion a cut. Eat it like any food; the jar comes back with the last bite. About 4 months in a fridge or the winter cold, 2 to 4 in a basement, 2 weeks in a summer pantry, and the first portion taken costs a third of what's left.
[/list]

[h2]Fish Guts, heads and stock[/h2]
A whole fish over 0.6 weight gives Fish Guts when gutted or packed. Gut Fish takes the head off a fish over 1.0 weight: a Fish Head for soups, stews or chum. [b]Fish Head Stock:[/b] a pot holding 1.5 L of water and a Fish Head make a stock; its first ingredient turns it into Fish Head Soup or Fish Head Stew, with the head taking no ingredient slot: up to 6 ingredients plus seasonings. [b]Bone Broth[/b] (2 Animal Bones, a Large one or 6 Small; the bones come back boiled, still good for tools) and [b]Chicken Stock[/b] (2 Chicken Feet or chicken and turkey heads) work the same way: Bone Broth Soup or Stew, Chicken Soup or Stew. Every stock's soup and stew is tastier than a plain one and takes the boredom off. A fish big enough to fillet can still be sliced at any salted, dried or rehydrated stage.

[h2]Black Pudding, Blood Sausage and Smoked Sausage[/h2]
[list]
[*]Bleed a carcass into a bucket: about 3.5 L for every 100 kg of animal. Fresh blood keeps a day, three in a fridge.
[*][b]Cure Blood[/b] (right-click the bucket) with salt or vinegar: 3 days, a week in a fridge.
[*][b]Mix Black Pudding[/b] in a baking or roasting pan: 1 L of blood, oats, rice, or 12 grains of barley, rye or wheat, half a block of fat, a pinch of salt. Bake an hour, turn it out, slice it.
[*]Butchering gives [b]Raw Casings[/b]; clean and salt them, [b]Stuff Blood Sausages[/b], and boil them half an hour in a pot of water: a week's keeping. Fry them too, or slice them.
[*]Hang boiled links in the Smokehouse for a day of cool-weather smoke: [b]Smoked Blood Sausage[/b], three weeks' keeping, eaten cold or fried.
[*][b]Stuff Sausages[/b] with pork, a little beef or venison, back fat and salt, then season them as you would a stew: garlic, onion, pepper, oregano, thyme, sage. Blood sausage links take seasoning too. Fry or boil them fresh, or hang them raw in the Smokehouse for six hours of a stoked fire: [b]Smoked Sausage[/b], two weeks' keeping.
[/list]

[h2]A little history[/h2]
The salt route is the lower Volga's: around Astrakhan, fish is salted in vats under a weighted lid, soaked, and dried in the shade, small fish whole and big ones split or hung from hooks. Stockfish is unsalted fish hung on open racks through the cold northern Norwegian spring and traded across Europe since the Viking age. In the charqueadas of southern Brazil, beef was salted in piles, washed and dried in the sun as charque. Turkish kavurma, French confit and the Balkan meso u masti are all meat cooked in fat and sealed in clay pots for the winter. The Smokehouse is the Kentucky farm smokehouse and the Bosnian pušnica, fed from a low firebox outside the wall; its winter smoke makes the Balkans' dry cured suho meso.

[h2]Works well with[/h2]
[b]Traditional Preservation (Salt)[/b] gives you a sustainable source of salt: one batch (5 Evaporated Salt, 25 uses) packs six half-weight panfish, or most of a 4 weight walleye. Traditional Preservation (Fish & Meat) needs no other mods besides Traditional Preservation (Core).

[h2]Sandbox options[/h2]
Set salt drying time, the slowest it can take, stockfish drying and maturing times, the ageing times of dry cured meat and Country Ham, the salt cure time and spoilage strictness. Turn butchering fat, charque and Potted Meat's keeping by place on or off, and set charque drying time.
