[h1]Traditional Preservation (Fish)[/h1]

Preserve your catch the old way. Salt it, press it and hang it to dry, or gut it and hang it in the winter cold. Dried fish keeps for months or even years, with no fridge and no power.

[b]Build 42 only (42.20 or newer). This is an early release, so back up your save before enabling it. Singleplayer only: multiplayer is not supported.[/b]

[b]Requires Traditional Preservation (Core)[/b]: subscribe to it too, and enable it in the Mods menu (it loads first).

[h2]Features[/h2]
[list]
[*]Two methods: salt drying all year, and cold drying in winter with no salt
[*]A salting tub and three drying stations, with their own art and two facings
[*]Each hanging stack has its own drying progress and look, so you can start and collect batches independently
[*]Rain, cold, humidity and the seasons all matter, and drying carries on while you're away
[*]Fish keep their species and size through every stage, with their own icons and ground models
[*]Gutting and packing give Fish Guts for chum, and gutting a big fish gives a Fish Head for the pot
[/list]

[h2]Which method should I use?[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]Salt drying[/b] can be done in any season, though weather slows it. It costs salt and needs a tub, and the fish keeps for 4 to 6 months and goes straight into soups and stews.
[*][b]Cold drying[/b] needs no salt or tub, and the fish keeps for 1 to 2 years. It only works in a cold season, and the fish must be rehydrated before use.
[/list]

[h2]Salt drying[/h2]

[h3]1. Pack Fish in Salt[/h3]
You need a raw fish or fillet, salt and a sharp knife or meat cleaver (kept). Salt goes by size: 4 for each started half weight of the packed fish (about 15% of its weight, as on the Volga), so a bluegill takes 4 and a 4.5 weight walleye 36. A vanilla shaker holds 10. A fish over 4.5 weight (about 2 kg) is too thick to salt whole: gut and split it, then pack the halves. Rotten, frozen and cooked fish can't be packed. Salt Packed fish only keeps for days.

[h3]2. Build a Salting Tub[/h3]
Carpentry 2, Fishing 2. Takes 4 planks, 6 nails, 2 twine, a hammer and a saw (both kept).

[h3]3. Press[/h3]
Load up to 50 weight of packed fish, 35 fish at most, and press Craft. It takes 1 in-game day for small fish (up to 1.1 weight) and up to 2 days with a fish of 2.2 weight or more in the batch, as the heaviest fish sets the time, and nothing else can go in until you've taken out the last pressed fish.

[h3]4. Hang it to dry[/h3]
Choose Hang Salt Pressed Fish to Dry on any drying station, load your pressed fish and press Craft. See Drying stations below.

[h3]5. Eat, cook or rehydrate[/h3]
Salt Dried fish is very salty, but can go straight into soup, stew, rice or pasta. Rehydrate Fish uses 1 L of water and gives a milder fish that cooks like fresh, but spoils in days.

[h2]Cold drying[/h2]

[h3]1. Gut Fish[/h3]
You need a raw whole fish and a sharp knife or meat cleaver (kept). Fillets can't be gutted. A Gutted Fish keeps like fresh fish and can still be cooked. A fish over 1.0 weight also gives a Fish Head.

[h3]2. Split Fish (if needed)[/h3]
A Gutted Fish over 2.5 weight can be cut into two Fish Halves, each with half the weight and food, to fit smaller stacks.

[h3]3. Hang it to dry[/h3]
Choose Hang Gutted Fish to Cold Dry on any drying station, load your fish and press Craft. Frozen fish is fine. The station must not be in a room: open air, or under an open roof (a porch or open shed) at three quarters of the speed.

[h3]4. Rehydrate[/h3]
Cold Dried fish is light but too hard to cook with. Rehydrate Fish uses 1 L of water and gives a fish you can cook, which spoils in days. Stockfish matures in storage over 60 days, from Unripe to Ripe. Ripe, it cheers you up once rehydrated, and so does the stew it goes into.

Changed your mind? A Gutted Fish or Fish Half can still be packed in salt.

[h2]Drying stations[/h2]
Each press of Craft hangs one stack, which keeps its spot until collected. Hover a stack to see its method, drying rate and any spoilage. A stack can't mix salted and gutted fish. Weights are inventory weight, not kilograms.

[h3]Fish Drying Frame[/h3]
Carpentry 2, Fishing 2. Takes 6 long sticks, 3 nails, 4 twine and a hammer (kept). Holds 8 stacks of up to 2.5 weight each. The only station that can be built in a room.

[h3]Fish Hanging Beam[/h3]
Carpentry 4, Fishing 2. Takes 6 planks, 6 nails, 1 large hook and a hammer (kept). Holds 4 stacks of up to 15 weight each. Open air or under an open roof, not in a room.

[h3]Fish Gantry[/h3]
Carpentry 6, Fishing 2. Takes 2 logs, 2 planks, 8 nails, 2 large hooks and a hammer (kept). Holds 2 stacks of up to 50 weight each. Open air or under an open roof, not in a room.

[h2]Weather and seasons[/h2]
Drying usually takes 12 to 55 in-game days.

[list]
[*][b]Salt drying:[/b] 12 days at best, at 20 C or warmer. Cool weather slows it, but frost doesn't stop it, so a batch still finishes in about a month. Damp air slows it a little.
[*][b]Cold drying:[/b] 32 days at best, between 0 C and 5 C in a good breeze. Frost, still air and mild weather slow it, and it pauses above 10 C. A batch hung November to January takes about 50 to 55 days.
[*][b]Fresh fish is fragile:[/b] hard frost or rain in a cold stack's first days takes a little off how long it keeps.
[*][b]Rain:[/b] pauses open stations until the fish has dried off. You lose time, not progress. Snow pauses salt drying but not cold drying.
[/list]

[h3]Under an open roof, and indoors[/h3]
[list]
[*][b]Under an open roof[/b] (a porch, an open shed, no room round it): rain never reaches it. Salt fish dries at the open-air speed, in the shade. Cold fish dries at three quarters of the speed.
[*][b]Indoors[/b] (a room, including one you build with walls all round and a full roof): salt drying runs at about two thirds of the speed, about 21 to 27 days. Cold drying stops, as it needs moving air.
[*][b]Hot sun:[/b] above 25 C, salt fish in the open air that is less than half dry loses a little keeping time. Shade spares it.
[*][b]A warm cure:[/b] if the season averages above 20 C while fish is in the tub, it sours by the spine and keeps less long.
[*][b]Heating a room doesn't help:[/b] it goes by the outdoor temperature.
[/list]

[h2]Spoilage[/h2]
Spoilage follows the average weather of about the last 10 days, so one warm afternoon in January won't hurt.

[list]
[*][b]Gutted fish and halves[/b] spoil once that average goes above 10 C. A stack rots in about 2 days, faster the warmer it gets, and comes off rotten.
[*][b]Salt fish[/b] only suffers when that average is above 22 C and the air is muggy: a few days in high summer, if any.
[/list]

[h3]Through the year[/h3]
On default weather: cold dry from November to mid-February (about 50 to 55 days). Late in February a batch can run into the spring; in October about 30% of cold batches rot, in March almost all, and from April to September all of them. Salt drying takes about 15 to 24 days in summer and a month in winter.

[h2]Weights and keeping times[/h2]
Weights are shares of the original whole fish. Days are fresh, then rotten.
[list]
[*][b]Salt Packed:[/b] 90%, 3 and 6 days
[*][b]Salt Pressed:[/b] 79 to 84%, 7 and 14 days
[*][b]Salt Dried:[/b] about 37%, 120 and 180 days
[*][b]Gutted Fish:[/b] 80% (90% for a fish too small to give a head), 3 and 6 days
[*][b]Cold Dried:[/b] about 18%, 365 and 730 days
[*][b]Rehydrated:[/b] about 53 to 66%, 2 and 4 days
[/list]

Rehydrating doesn't bring back calories lost to drying. Salt fish keeps about 90% and cold dried about 95%, less the 6% a Fish Head takes.

[h2]Eating uncooked[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]Salt Packed:[/b] can't be eaten
[*][b]Salt Pressed, Gutted Fish and Fish Halves:[/b] raw, so cook them first or risk getting sick
[*][b]Salt Dried, Cold Dried and Rehydrated:[/b] can be eaten without cooking
[/list]

[h2]Fish Guts, heads and slicing[/h2]
A whole fish over 0.6 weight gives one Fish Guts when gutted or packed, weighing 10% of the fish, and loses that weight. Small fish, fillets and salmon give none. A fish big enough to fillet can still be sliced at any salted, dried or rehydrated stage.

Gut Fish also takes the head off. A fish over 1.0 weight gives a Fish Head: 10% of its weight and 6% of its food, for soups, stews or chum. Salted fish keep their heads.

[b]Fish Head Stock:[/b] a pot holding 1.5 L of water and a Fish Head make a pot of stock that is already a stew, with the head taking no ingredient slot. Add up to 6 stew ingredients plus seasonings, and it starts with the same mood boost as a plain stew. Divide it into 4 or 2 bowls at a table, as with any stew.

[h2]A little history[/h2]
Stockfish is unsalted cod hung on open racks through the cold northern Norwegian spring, traded across Europe since the Viking age. Salt cod (klippfisk, bacalhau) is salted, pressed and dried in mild air, and from the 1500s it fed sailors and Catholic fast days on both sides of the Atlantic. On the lower Volga around Astrakhan, large salted fish such as pike are hung from hooks to dry.

[h2]Works well with[/h2]
[b]Traditional Preservation (Salt)[/b] gives you a sustainable source of salt: one batch (5 Evaporated Salt, 25 uses) packs a dozen panfish, or one 4 weight walleye with salt to spare. Traditional Preservation (Fish) needs no other mods besides Traditional Preservation (Core).
