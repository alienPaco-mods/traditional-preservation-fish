## 1. What This Mod Does

You catch more fish than you can eat. This mod turns that surplus into food that keeps for **months (salted)** or **up to two years (cold dried)**, using the two great preserved fish of history: Portuguese *bacalhau* (salt cod) and Norwegian *stockfish* (air-dried in the cold). No power, no fridge, just salt, wood and weather.

---

## 2. Quick Start

> **Requires Traditional Preservation (Core)**, the series' shared mod (TP Core for short). Enable it alongside this one; it loads first.

**Summer catch?** Pack it in salt → press it in a **Salting Tub** → hang it on a **Fish Drying Frame**.
**Winter catch?** Gut it → hang it on the frame. No salt needed.

| You need | For |
|---|---|
| Sharp knife or meat cleaver (kept) | Packing, gutting, splitting, slicing |
| 4 salt per half weight of fish | Salt route only |
| Carpentry 2, Fishing 2 | Tub and frame |
| 1 L water per fish | Rehydrating to cook |

## 3. The Two Roads: Salt or Cold

| | **Salt route** | **Cold route** |
|---|---|---|
| Season | Any (best in summer) | **Winter only** (about -10 to 5 C) |
| Salt | 4 per half weight of fish | None |
| Keeps | About 4–6 months | About 1–2 years |
| Eating | Straight into soup, stew, rice or pasta (salty) | Must be rehydrated first |
| Weight | About 37% of the fresh fish | About 18%, the lightest food you can carry |

**Rule of thumb:** salt in summer, cold in winter. The frame takes both kinds at once, in separate stacks.

## 4. Walkthrough: The Salt Route

**Step 1. Pack Fish in Salt** (crafting menu)
Raw fish or fillet + salt by its size + knife (kept) → **Salt Packed** fish.
- Fresh, stale, even **part-eaten** fish works. Rotten, frozen or cooked doesn't.
- **Big fish get split first:** a whole or gutted fish over **4.5 weight** (about 2 kg) is too thick to salt whole. **Gut Fish**, then **Split Fish**, and pack the halves. Bream-sized fish and smaller go in whole, heads on.
- **Salt goes by weight:** 4 for each started half weight of the packed fish, about 15% of its weight. Carry it: only the first 2 can come from a container nearby.

| Fish | Packed weight | Salt |
|---|---|---|
| Bluegill, 0.5 | 0.5 | 4 |
| Walleye, 4.0 | 3.6 | 32 |
| Walleye, 4.5 (the most salted whole) | 4.05 | 36 |
| Catfish, 10.0: too big, gut and split | each half 4.0 | 32 a half |
| Vanilla fillet of that catfish, 0.5 | 4.5 | 36 |

- Any salt works: a vanilla shaker holds **10**, the Salt mod's Evaporated Salt **5** and a Bag of Salt **50**. Part-used salt goes first.

> ✏ *Margin note:* on the Volga a salter reckons 1 to 1.5 kg of salt for 10 kg of vobla; salt cod took up to a third of its weight. The mod charges about 15%. A split catfish costs you more than a whole Bag of Salt, which is why the Salt mod's lick is worth the walk.
- A whole fish over 0.6 weight also gives **Fish Guts** (see Secrets).
- Packed fish keeps only days. Get it in the tub.

**Step 2. Press in the Salting Tub**
Load up to **50 weight / 35 items**, press **Craft**. The board and stones go on for **1 to 2 in-game days**: the **heaviest fish** sets the time, from 1 day (every fish up to 1.1 weight, about half a kilo) to 2 days (any fish of 2.2 weight, about a kilo, or more). Press small fish together and they come out a day sooner. The tub stays closed until you've taken every pressed fish out.
- **Mind a warm spell.** When the last ten days average above 20 C, fish in the tub sours by the spine before the salt reaches it, and the finished fish keeps less long: about a seventh off for a day at 21 C, over a third for two days at 25 C. Hover the batch to see it. Salt in spring and autumn, or press small fish.

**Optional: rinse off the salt**
Right-click Salt Pressed fish and open its submenu: **Rinse Off Salt** is a recipe that takes **0.15 L of clean water** a fish from a bottle or pot you carry (the Craft window does a whole batch). Standing near a sink, bathtub, barrel or well? **Rinse at Sink** in the same submenu rinses every selected fish there. Tainted water won't do. The dried fish comes out **less salty** (a third less thirst, no sour note) but keeps **a tenth less long**: less salt, less protection. Skip it and nothing changes.

> 📜 *On the Volga, vobla makers soak the pressed fish for about an hour per day it spent in salt before hanging it in the shade. Too much salt left on the skin crusts over and makes a fish you'll be drinking water after all evening.*

**Step 3. Hang on the Fish Drying Frame**
Pick **Hang Salt Pressed Fish to Dry**, load pressed fish, press **Craft**. Each press hangs **one stack** of up to 2.5 weight, which dries on its own timer. There are **8 stacks** per frame, so you can keep adding while others dry.

**Step 4. Eat or rehydrate**
**Salt Dried** fish is safe to eat and goes straight into soups and stews. **Rehydrate Fish** (1 L water, container kept) makes a mild fish that cooks like fresh.

> ⚠ Packed and pressed fish are **not** safe uncooked. Only drying makes salt fish safe.

## 5. Walkthrough: The Cold Route

1. **Gut Fish**: whole raw fish + knife → **Gutted Fish** (+ guts, and a **Fish Head** from a fish over 1.0 weight). Fillets can't be gutted.
2. **Split Fish** (optional): a gutted fish over 2.5 weight can be split into two **Fish Halves**.
3. **Hang**: pick **Hang Gutted Fish to Cold Dry** on the frame. Frozen fish is fine.
4. **Let it mature**: stockfish ripens in storage over **60 days**, in any container (a fridge slows it, a freezer stops it). The name reads *(Cold Dried, Unripe)* until it's *(Cold Dried, Ripe)*, and the tooltip shows the percentage.
   **Stockfish Pallet** (build menu: hammer, saw, 3 planks, 6 nails, Carpentry 2): stack your stockfish on it the way the warehouses did. In a **loft** (a room upstairs) it ripens **twice as fast** (30 days); in a **ground-floor room**, half as fast again (40 days). Covered, outdoors or down in a damp basement it does no better than a shelf. It holds 20 weight, and the stack on it grows from a third to full as you load it, as long as nothing but stockfish is on it.

5. **Rehydrate**: Cold Dried fish must be soaked (1 L water) before it can go into recipes. Ripe stockfish cheers you up (unhappiness -10), and so does the stew it goes into. Unripe, it's dull (+10). Halfway, it's neither.

> ⚠ Cold drying **rots** if the season warms up. Hang from November to mid-February. See the Almanac.

## 7. Stages at a Glance

| Stage | Weight* | Food value | Fresh / rotten days |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salt Packed | 90% | 100% | 3 / 6 |
| Salt Pressed | 79–84% | 100% | 7 / 14 |
| **Salt Dried** | ~37% | 90% | **120 / 180** (rinsed: 108 / 162) |
| Rehydrated (salt) | ~66% | 90% | 2 / 4 |
| Gutted | 80% | 94% | 3 / 6 |
| **Cold Dried** | ~18% | 89% | **365 / 730** |
| Rehydrated (cold) | ~53% | 89% | 2 / 4 |
| Fish Head | 10% | 6% | 3 / 6 |

\*Compared with the whole fresh fish. Gutting removes the guts (10%) and, from a fish over 1.0 weight, the head. A smaller fish keeps its head, so its gutted weight is 90% with all of its food. Fish under 0.6 weight have no guts to remove and keep all their weight.

## 8. The Fisherman's Almanac (Seasons)

Drying runs on real weather, recorded every 10 minutes, **even while you're away**.

- **Salt drying**: 12 days at 20 C or warmer in dry air. Cold slows it down, even frost, but never stops it.
- **Cold drying**: 32 days at full speed, between 0 and 5 C with a good wind. Slower in frost (four fifths at -5 C, three fifths below), slower from 5 to 10 C, and it stops above 10 C. **Wind** matters as much: still air halves the speed, a good breeze (the popup's *Good drying wind*) adds a fifth. A Kentucky winter's wind is mostly light, so a batch hung November to January takes about 50 to 55 days. Hang by mid-February at the latest.
- **Fresh fish is fragile.** Until a cold stack is about 30% dried it has no dry skin: hard frost (below -5 C) and lying wet above freezing each take a little off how long the finished fish keeps, a quarter at most. Rot from a warm season also hits a fresh stack hardest and a nearly dry one hardly at all.
- **Rain** pauses open stations until the fish dries off. Progress is **paused, never lost**. Snow pauses salt fish but not cold fish, and cold fish has to dry off after rain too.
- **Where it hangs** (the popup's *Place*):
  - **Open air**: no roof overhead. Walls with no roof are still the open air.
  - **Under an open roof**: a porch, an open shed, a floor laid on the level above. Rain never reaches it. Salt fish dries as fast as in the open, **in the shade**. Cold fish dries at three quarters of the speed, as the roof breaks some of the wind, but never waits out the rain.
  - **Indoors**: inside a room, including one you built yourself with walls all round and a full roof. Salt drying runs at about two thirds of the speed; cold drying **stops**, as the air is still.
- **Hot sun**: above 25 C, salt fish in the open air that is less than half dry takes a little off how long it keeps (the popup says *Too hot in the sun*). A few percent in a Kentucky summer; hang it under an open roof and it's spared.

| Feb | 29–33 d | 27 d | 27 d | 52–93 d | 63–101 d | 3% | 16% | 0% |

## 9. Secrets & Hidden Features

- 🐟 **Fish keep their identity.** A *Small Largemouth Bass* stays one through every stage, so you get *Salt Dried Small Largemouth Bass*. Each fish keeps its own weight and calories, so a batch of mixed sizes comes out mixed too.
- 🎨 **Icons change with the cure.** Pale with salt when pressed, browned when salt dried, grey when cold dried, darker again when rehydrated. Gutted fish are drawn **without their heads**, as stockfish were. Halves show red flesh and a backbone.
- 🪝 **Fish Guts are free chum.** Packing or gutting a whole fish over 0.6 weight gives Fish Guts weighing 10% of the fish, the same size limit vanilla uses for filleting. Smaller fish, fillets and salmon give none.
- 🐟 **Fish Heads.** Gutting a fish over 1.0 weight takes its head off, as stockfish makers did: 10% of the weight and 6% of the food go with it. Salted fish keep their heads. A head spoils like fresh fish, makes you sick raw, is a little grim even cooked, and can't be dried. Put it in a soup, a stew or chum, or better, make stock.
- 🍲 **Fish Head Stock.** **Create Fish Head Stock** (Craft menu of the head or the pot, anywhere): a Cooking or Forged Pot with 1.5 L of water + a Fish Head. It's a stew from the start, and the head takes no slot, so you still get **6 stew ingredients** plus seasonings. It starts with the same mood boost as a plain stew (-20 unhappiness), and is named for what you add: *Fish Head Stock with Fish and Carrots*, or plain *Fish Head Stock* past 3 kinds, as vanilla stew falls back to *Stew*. Cook it, then **Divide Food into 4 Bowls** or **2 Bowls** at a table or counter, as with any stew.
- 🔪 **Fillet at any stage.** A whole fish over 0.6 weight can still be cut into 2 fillets after it's been salted, dried or rehydrated, just like a fresh one. The fillets stay at the same stage.
- 🧂 **Changed your mind?** A Gutted Fish or Fish Half can still be packed in salt. It won't give a second lot of guts.
- 🌫 **Damp air matters a little.** Air more humid than 50% slows salt drying, though the game's air is rarely that damp.
- 🔥 **Summer spoilage is gentle.** Salt fish only loses keeping time when the last 10 days averaged over 22 C *and* the air is humid: a few days in a hot July, if any.
- 🎲 **Every frame looks different.** Each stack picks its hanging look at random (whole, headless or split), so a full frame looks hand-hung.
- 📊 **Hover a hanging stack** to see its method, progress, time left, the temperature, how wet it is and how fast it's drying right now, plus a warning if the season is spoiling it.
- 🥄 **Salt Dried fish needs no soaking for soup.** Toss it straight into a pot.

## 9½. The Fisherman's Logbook

*Found in a tackle box, the pages stiff with salt and smelling faintly of cod.*

### Food you can carry

Drying takes the water out and leaves the food in. Here's what one unit of inventory weight is worth, made from a fish big enough to give guts and a head:

| Stage | Calories per 1.0 weight | Against fresh |
|---|---|---|
| Fresh fish | about 400 | × 1 |
| Salt Dried | about 975 | × 2½ |
| Cold Dried | about 2,000 | × 5 |
| Rehydrated (cold) | about 670 | back to heavy |
| Fish Head | about 240 | it's mostly bone |

> ✏ *Margin note:* every vanilla fish carries the same calories for its weight, about 400 per 1.0 inventory weight. So a stack of Cold Dried fish weighing 2.5 holds as much food as about 12 weight of fresh fish. Rehydrate it at the pot, not on the road.

> ✏ *Margin note:* a Medium Walleye of 4 weight is about 1,600 calories. Its head takes about 95 of them, which is about right for the cheeks, tongue and gelatin, and why it belongs in the stock pot.

### Why it works the way it does

- **Why does salt dry fish?** Salt draws water out through the flesh (osmosis), and bacteria can't live in what's left. That's why packed fish keeps only days, pressed fish a week or two, and fully dried fish for months.
- **Why press it?** The board and stones squeeze out the brine the salt has pulled from the fish, so drying starts from a firmer, drier fish. Norwegian and Portuguese salt cod makers stacked their fish under weights for the same reason.
- **Why does cold drying stop indoors?** Stockfish dries in moving air at around freezing: cold enough that nothing rots, dry and windy enough that the water leaves. In a room the air goes still; under an open roof some wind still gets through.
- **Why dry salt fish in the shade?** In full summer sun the skin dries hard before the inside does, trapping the wet, and the fat turns rusty. Volga salters hang their vobla under awnings and in open sheds.
- **Why do warm stacks rot?** Unsalted fish has nothing protecting it but the cold. Once the season averages above 10 C, it spoils faster than it dries.
- **Why gut it first?** The guts spoil first and fastest. Stockfish makers gutted and headed every fish before it went on the rack.

### Old names, old ways

- **Stockfish** comes from the Lofoten islands of northern Norway, hung on wooden racks called *hjell* from February to May. It was traded across Europe from the Viking age on, and keeps for years.
- **Stockfish was never eaten off the rack.** After about three months on the *hjell*, it was stacked on pallets in dry, airy warehouses for two or three more months to mature, then sorted by hand into a dozen or more grades for Italy, Nigeria and the rest of the trade.
- **The heads weren't wasted.** Norway still dries its stockfish heads separately, and ships them to West Africa, where they're prized for soups and stews.
- **Bacalhau** (Portuguese) and **klippfisk** (Norwegian) are salt cod, salted, pressed and dried on rocks in the sun. Portugal is said to have a different bacalhau recipe for every day of the year.
- **On the lower Volga** around Astrakhan, large salted fish such as pike are hung from hooks to dry, and smaller ones dried whole with their heads on.

## 10. Tips from the Docks

- **Fillets don't cheat the salt.** A vanilla fillet weighs 5% of its fish but holds half its food, so salted it pays and weighs as half the gutted fish. Fillets cut from a cured fish weigh half of it.
- **Big catch?** A fish heavier than a stack's limit won't fit. Split it in two, or hang it on the Beam or Gantry.
- **An open shed beats a room.** A frame under a porch or lean-to roof dries salt fish at full speed, rain or shine, in the shade. Keep the room for when you have nowhere else.
- **Winter is stockfish season.** Gut everything you catch in December and January. The lightest, longest-keeping food in the game. Turn the heads into stock the same day, as they spoil like fresh fish.
- **Rehydrate only what you'll cook today.** Rehydrated fish keeps just 2 days.
- **Don't eat December's stockfish in January.** Give it the 60 days. A crate in the shed is enough, and the freezer is the one place it won't ripen.
- With the **Salt mod**, a salt lick keeps you packing forever. See the Collection Guide.

## 11. Troubleshooting

| Problem | Why |
|---|---|
| Tub won't accept more | It's full (50 weight or 35 items), pressing, or still has pressed fish in it |
| Frame refuses a fish | It takes only Salt Pressed fish, or Gutted Fish and halves. Raw, frozen salted, rotten and cooked fish are refused, and a frame stack holds 2.5 weight at most |
| Cold stack came out rotten | It was hung in a warm season: the last 10 days averaged above 10 C |
| Cold dried fish keeps less than a year | Hard frost or rain caught it in its first days on the frame |
| Not drying | Rain on an open frame (cold fish too, until it dries off); or cold drying indoors |
| Salt fish keeps less than it should | A warm cure (the season was above 20 C while it pressed) or hot sun on an open frame before it was half dry |
| Time Remaining looks wrong in the window | The window shows the time in the best weather, or the longest press. Hover the stack or the pressing batch for the real time left |
| Pack Fish in Salt won't take a fish | A whole or gutted fish over 4.5 weight must be split first; or you don't carry enough salt for its size |
| No Fish Head from gutting | The fish was 1.0 weight or less, or it was packed in salt (salted fish keep their heads) |
| Create Fish Head Stock missing | The pot needs the full 1.5 L of water |
| Can't divide the stock into bowls | Like vanilla stew, it needs a table or counter nearby, and enough bowls |

## 13. Appendix: The Tester's Toolkit

*For testers, modders and the curious. These commands exist only when the game is started in debug mode (Steam launch option `-debug`). Type them into the in-game **Command Console** and press Enter. `TPF.help()` lists them in the game.*

> ⚠ They change your save directly, so try them on a test save, not a playthrough you care about.

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `TPF.weather()` | The drying weather now: temperature, the 10-day season average, humidity, wetness, the wind, the drying speed open, under an open roof, indoors and cold, whether unsalted and salted fish are spoiling, and the running totals. |
| `TPF.age(hours)` | Gives every hanging stack this many hours of perfect drying weather. `TPF.age(288)` finishes a salt stack, `TPF.age(768)` a cold one. |
| `TPF.spoil(hours)` | Adds spoiling weather, to test rotting. A fresh cold stack rots after 48. |
| `TPF.frost(hours)` | Adds hours of hard frost, to test early frost on a fresh cold stack (72 hours take a tenth of its keeping). |
| `TPF.heat(hours)` | Adds hours of hot sun, to test it on a fresh open salt stack (24 hours take a twentieth of its keeping). |
| `TPF.warmCure(hours)` | Adds hours of a warm cure at 25 C, to test souring in a running tub (24 hours take about a fifth). |
| `TPF.dry()` | Clears the wetness, so open and cold frames start drying again at once after rain. |
| `TPF.fish(name, weight, n)` | Spawns `n` whole fish at that weight, with food scaled to match, e.g. `TPF.fish("Walleye", 4, 2)`. `TPF.fish()` lists the names. |
| `TPF.heads(n)` | Spawns Fish Heads (default food values). |
| `TPF.stock()` | Spawns a Pot of Fish Head Stock (default values, no head listed). |
| `TPF.salt(n)` | Spawns vanilla Salt shakers. |

The Salting Tub runs on the game clock, not the weather, so `TPF.age` doesn't speed up pressing. Use the game's own time controls for that.

**Typical check of the whole salt route:** `TPF.fish("Walleye", 4, 3)` and `TPF.salt(10)` (each 4 weight walleye takes 32 salt, and a shaker holds 10), pack and press, then hang the fish and `TPF.age(288)`.

**Typical check of heads:** `TPF.fish("Walleye", 4)` and `TPF.fish("Bluegill", 0.9)`, then Gut Fish on both. Only the walleye gives a head.
