# Traditional Preservation (Fish & Meat) — Build 42 prototype (v0.4.8)

**Status: experimental.** The stations build, open and hang fish in the game, but a full year hasn't been played. Back up your save before enabling.

## History

The mod's two routes are the two great preserved fish of the North Atlantic. Stockfish is unsalted cod hung on open racks through the cold northern Norwegian spring, and it has been traded across Europe since the Viking age. Salt cod (Norwegian *klippfisk*, Portuguese *bacalhau*) is salted, pressed and dried in mild air, and from the 1500s it fed sailors and Catholic fast days on both sides of the Atlantic. On the lower Volga around Astrakhan, large salted fish such as pike are hung from hooks to dry. Both keep for months or years, and both are soaked before cooking. The meat route follows Brazil's *charqueadas*: beef salted in piles, washed and dried in the summer sun as *charque*, always desalted before it is cooked.

## Install

Copy this folder to `~/Zomboid/mods/TraditionalPreservationFishMeat`, as a real copy rather than a symlink. It needs the **TP Core** mod (`TraditionalPreservationCore`, the series' shared mod) installed and enabled the same way, and no other mods.

## How it works

The salt route can be done in any season. The cold route needs no salt, keeps about three times as long and only works in a cold season. Both use the same drying stations.

**Salt route**

1. **Pack Fish in Salt**: a raw fish or fillet, salt by its size and a sharp knife or meat cleaver (kept). Any fish works, fresh or stale and even part-eaten, but not rotten, frozen or cooked. It takes 4 salt for each started half weight of the packed fish, about 15% of its weight (Volga salters use 10 to 15%): a bluegill takes 4, a 4 weight walleye 32, a 4.5 weight walleye 36 (packed weight is after the guts come out). A whole or gutted fish over 4.5 weight (about 2 kg) is too thick to salt whole, as on the Volga: gut it, split it and pack the halves (a 10 weight catfish gives two halves of 4.0, 32 salt each). Salt in a container in reach covers the first 2; the rest must be in your inventory (bags count). Any salt works: a vanilla shaker holds 10, an Evaporated Salt from Traditional Preservation (Salt) 5 and a Bag of Salt 50, and part-used salt goes first. Gives one Salt Packed fish, which keeps for days, not weeks, and Fish Guts if the fish has any (see below). The same limits apply when loading the tub and the frame. A Gutted Fish or Fish Half can be packed too, and goes through the salt route the same way, so you can gut a fish and still salt it.
2. **Build a Salting Tub** (Carpentry 2, Fishing 2): 4 planks, 6 nails, 2 twine, a hammer and a saw (both kept).
3. **Cure**: pick **Cure Salted Fish**, load up to 50 weight of packed fish, 35 items at most, into the tub and press **Craft**. The board and stones go on until the salt has gone through the thickest fish, so the heaviest fish sets the time: 1 in-game day if every fish is up to 1.1 weight (about 0.5 kg), 2 days with any fish of 2.2 weight (about 1 kg) or more, and in between by weight. Big fish are split before salting (over 4.5 weight), so 2 days is the longest. The tub is closed from then until the last Salt Cured fish is taken out, so nothing can be added. The clock keeps running while you are away.
   *Optional rinse*: before hanging, **Rinse Off Salt** (a recipe in the fish's submenu, 0.15 L of clean carried water a fish, batches in the Craft window, works in the dark) or **Rinse at Sink** in the same submenu (a sink, bathtub, barrel or well of clean water within 3 tiles) soaks the excess salt off, as on the Volga. The fish is marked Rinsed: two thirds of the thirst, a tenth less keeping, and its Salt Dried fish has thirst 20 instead of 30 and no unhappiness. Tainted water is refused. Skipping it changes nothing.
4. **Build a Fish Drying Frame** (Carpentry 2, Fishing 2): 6 long sticks, 3 nails, 4 twine and a hammer (kept).
5. **Hang**: pick Hang Salt Cured Fish to Dry in the frame's recipe list, load cured fish and press **Craft**. Each press hangs one stack, which dries on its own timer. A stack holds 2.5 weight, so a full frame holds 20, and a fish heavier than 2.5 has to be sliced first (Slice Fish, two fillets of half its weight each) or hung on a bigger station. You can keep adding stacks while others dry, up to the 8 fixed spots, and finished stacks hold their spot until collected. Each sprite changes colour and thins down in five steps as its stack dries. Each stack picks its look at random when it starts, whatever is in it, so a frame doesn't look all alike. A salt stack is usually a whole fish and sometimes a headless or split one, and a cold stack is usually headless and sometimes split.
6. **Eat, cook or rehydrate**: Salt Dried fish keeps for about 4 to 6 months. It is very salty, but can go straight into soup, stew, rice or pasta. **Rehydrate Fish** uses 1 L of water and gives a mild, perishable fish that cooks like a fresh one and is safe without cooking, though it stays darker than fresh fish, as real soaked fish does. Packed and cured fish aren't safe yet: only drying makes salt fish safe to eat uncooked.

**Bigger fish**: two outdoor stations hang fish the same way as the frame, with the same recipes, rates and spoilage, but take far bigger stacks and show them larger on their hooks. They can be built in the open or under a roof with no room round it (a porch, an open shed), not in a room, and one picked up can't be put down in a room either.

- **Fish Hanging Beam** (Carpentry 4, Fishing 2): 6 planks, 6 nails, 1 large hook and a hammer (kept). 4 stacks of up to 15 weight each.
- **Fish Gantry** (Carpentry 6, Fishing 2): 2 logs, 2 planks, 8 nails, 2 large hooks and a hammer (kept). 2 stacks of up to 50 weight each.

**Cold route**

1. **Gut Fish**: a raw whole fish and a sharp knife or meat cleaver (kept). Gives a Gutted Fish, which keeps like a fresh fish, must be cooked before eating like one, and Fish Guts and a Fish Head if it has them. Fillets can't be gutted, so they stay on the salt route.
2. **Split Fish**: a Gutted Fish over 2.5 weight, too heavy for a frame stack, can be cut into two Fish Halves with a knife. Each keeps half the weight and food, and the fish's name, such as Medium Walleye Half. Fish that are too heavy for the frame can also hang whole on a Fish Hanging Beam or Fish Gantry.
3. **Hang**: pick Hang Gutted Fish to Cold Dry in the frame's recipe list, load gutted fish or halves and press **Craft**. Frozen fish is fine. The hanging fish fade to grey as they dry. A stack can't mix gutted and salted fish, but a frame can hold both kinds of stack.
4. **Mature**: stockfish ripens in storage, as it did for two or three months in the warehouses after the racks. It takes 60 days on the fish's own food clock: any container works, a fridge slows it and a freezer stops it. The name says *Unripe* or *Ripe*, and the tooltip shows how far along it is (Maturing: 40 percent). Fish made before version 0.3.3 count as ripe. A **Stockfish Pallet** (build menu: Carpentry 2, 3 planks, 6 nails, hammer and saw kept; a 20 weight container) speeds it up where the warehouses kept stockfish: a loft (a room upstairs) ×2, 30 days, a ground-floor room ×1.5, 40 days; covered, outdoors or in a basement no faster than a shelf. Right-click it to see its speed there. It shows a stack of stockfish, a third, two thirds or full by the weight on it, while it holds nothing but stockfish. A **Stockfish Bale** carries and stores it: right-click selected stockfish, *Tie a Bale*, with one use of Twine or a Rope. It holds 10 weight of stockfish and nothing else (weight reduction 30), the fish keep ripening inside, on a pallet too, and *Untie Bale* gives the fish and the cord back.
5. **Rehydrate**: Cold Dried fish keeps for about 1 to 2 years and weighs very little. It is as hard as wood: it can't be eaten or go into recipes until **Rehydrate Fish** has soaked it (0.4.4: no Eat option before), which uses 1 L of water. The soaked fish keeps how ripe it was: unripe it's dull (unhappiness +10), halfway it's plain, ripe it cheers you up (-10), and a soup or stew takes on its share.

**Fish Guts**: Gut Fish and Pack Fish in Salt both give one Fish Guts from a whole fish over 0.6 weight, the same size limit vanilla uses for filleting. The guts weigh 10% of the fish, and the fish loses that weight. Smaller fish, fillets and salmon give none and lose nothing. Filleting a fish later gives no more.

**Fish Head**: Gut Fish takes the head off, as stockfish makers did, and a whole fish over 1.0 weight gives a Fish Head named for its fish, such as Medium Walleye Head. It weighs 10% of the fish and takes 6% of its food, which the gutted fish loses. The limit is above the guts' 0.6 so small fish can't be farmed for heads. A head spoils like fresh fish (3 days fresh, 6 before rotting), makes you sick if eaten raw, and is a little unpleasant to eat even cooked. It goes into vanilla soups and stews or chum, and can't be dried. Pack Fish in Salt leaves the head on, as salted fish on the Volga were dried, so it gives no head.

**Fish Head Stock**: **Create Fish Head Stock** (Cooking, in hand, from the right-click Craft menu of the head or the pot) takes a Cooking Pot or Forged Pot holding 1.5 L of water and a Fish Head, and gives a Pot of Fish Head Stock. Unlike a vanilla stew, which only becomes a stew when its first ingredient goes in and that ingredient takes one of its 6 slots, the stock is a stew from the start and the head takes no slot. It takes the same ingredients as vanilla stew from the right-click menu under **Fish Head Stock**: **6 ingredients**, plus seasonings, which as in vanilla don't take a slot and go in once each. The head is listed among the ingredients in the tooltip. The pot is named for what went in, as a vanilla stew is but with the stock first: *Fish Head Stock with Fish and Carrots*. As in vanilla, two different foods of one type are named by the type (*with Vegetables*), seasonings are named only while there's room, and past 3 kinds it's just *Fish Head Stock*. The head itself isn't named. It starts with the same mood boost as a vanilla stew (unhappiness -20), and ingredients and seasonings change it from there as they do in a stew. It carries the head's food, must be cooked like a stew, keeps 3 days fresh and 5 before rotting, and gives the pot back when finished. **Divide Food into 4 Bowls** and **Divide Food into 2 Bowls** share it into bowls or clay bowls as vanilla does with a stew, and like vanilla's they need a table or counter nearby, giving Stew Bowls and the empty pot back.

**Slice Fish**: a whole fish over 0.6 weight can still be filleted with a sharp knife after it's been salted, dried or rehydrated, as a fresh one can. It gives 2 fillets at the same stage, each with half the fish's food and half its weight. (Vanilla's raw fillet is 5% of its fish: a cured fish has no guts left to lose.) Fillets and smaller fish can't be cut.

Fish keep their size and species through every stage, with the stage in brackets after the name and vanilla's freshness after that: *Gutted Small Largemouth Bass (Salt Cured) (Fresh)*, then *(Salt Dried)*, or on the cold route *(Cold Dried, Unripe)*, then *(Cold Dried, Ripe)* and *(Rehydrated, Cold Dried, Ripe)*. Gutted and Half are part of the fish's name: "Gutted" stays on every stage of a fish that went through Gut Fish (it's the headless one). A fish packed whole keeps its head and isn't called gutted, though packing takes the guts of one over 0.6 weight. Names follow the days: they update every in-game hour for what you carry and when an inventory window shows a container. They also keep the original fish's icon, pale with salt once Salt Cured, browned once Salt Dried, grey once Cold Dried and a darker brown or grey once rehydrated. A Gutted Fish is drawn without its head, as stockfish were, and stays headless through every later stage, and a Fish Half is that same fish split open, with red flesh and a backbone down the body and its fins and tail in the species' colours, and stays split through every later stage. Salted fish keep their heads, as small salted fish often do. Each fish also keeps its own weight and food, so a batch of mixed sizes comes out mixed too.

| Stage | Weight | Calories and nutrients | Hunger | Fresh / rotten days |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Salt Packed | 90% | 100% | 100% | 3 / 6 |
| Salt Cured | 79–84% | 100% | 100% | 7 / 14 |
| Salt Dried | about 37% | 90% | 70% | 120 / 180 |
| Rehydrated (salt) | about 66% | 90% | 100% | 2 / 4 |
| Gutted | 80% | 94% | 94% | 3 / 6 |
| Cold Dried | about 18% | 89% | 66% | 365 / 730 |
| Rehydrated (cold) | about 53% | 89% | 94% | 2 / 4 |

Percentages are of the whole fresh fish. They assume the fish was big enough to give guts (over 0.6 weight) and, on the cold route, a head (over 1.0). A fish too small for guts keeps that tenth of its weight at every stage. A cold-route fish of 1.0 weight or less keeps its head, so it's 90% when gutted, with all of its food. Curing takes a random 7 to 12% of each fish's weight. Freshness carries over as a share of each stage's fresh days, so a fish half way to stale is still half way to stale when gutted or packed. The cure starts the clock afresh, less what a warm cure took (see Seasons). Rehydrating carries the dried fish's share over, so an old dried fish gives a stale rehydrated one. A fillet vanilla cut counts as the food it holds once salted: it pays half its gutted fish's salt and comes out of the salt that heavy (9 times its raw weight).

### Fat, Lard and Tallow (0.4.1)

Vanilla butchering gives no fat and its Lard is loot only. `TraditionalPreservationFishMeatFat.lua` adds one row to vanilla's butchering parts tables (`AnimalPartsDefinitions`) when a world starts: **Pork Fat** for sows and boars (base 2-3), **Beef Suet** for cows and bulls (3-4), **Mutton Fat** for ewes and rams (1-2). Young animals and deer get none. Vanilla's own butchering code scales a row like any other part: times the animal's size and meat ratio, x2.8 on a Butchering Hook or x0.6 on the ground, a higher maximum with Butchering skill, half for roadkill, less for an old carcass, never under one. So a pig on the Hook gives 5 to 7 Pork Fat and one on the ground gives 1; a cow 8 to 10 and 1 or 2; a sheep 2 to 4 and 1.

Rendering needs no station. **Put Pork Fat in a Pot** / **Put Suet in a Pot** takes an empty Pot or Forged Pot and 2 raw pieces (suet and mutton fat mix). The pot cooks on any stove or fire in 60 minutes and burns at 150. **Pour Off Lard** / **Pour Off Tallow** give the pot back holding 0.5 L of the fluid `MeltedLard` or `MeltedTallow` (filled by the recipe's OnCreate, `F.onPourOffLard` / `F.onPourOffTallow`). **Strain Lard** / **Strain Tallow** take 0.5 L from any container, and need a Cheese Cloth, Dish Towel, Rag, Sheet or Strainer (kept), for vanilla `Base.Lard` or the new **Tallow** (vanilla Lard's values and its `base:bakingfat` tag) and one **Cracklings**. A burnt pot only takes **Scrape Out Burnt Fat**, which returns the pot.

The cap rule: two pieces make one block, so a pig's Lard (2 or 3 on the Hook) is worth about its meat and no more. A piece of raw fat has half the calories of a block and its cracklings (2170), keeps 2 / 4 days like raw meat, and is dangerous uncooked.

**In the Hearth's copper (0.4.2).** TP Core's copper holds fluids, so a piece of raw fat goes in as 0.25 L of a raw fluid (`RawPorkFat`, `RawSuet`: suet and mutton fat share one) by **Put Fat in the Copper** on the Hearth's menu (`TPFMFatCopper`, `...FatUI.lua`). Kept steaming (85 C or more) for an hour in all it becomes `MeltedLard` or `MeltedTallow` (`Hearth.turnsHot`), a real fluid that Fill from Copper puts in any container; **Strain Lard** / **Strain Tallow** take 0.5 L for a block and a Cracklings, so two pieces make a block as in a pot. The copper never burns fat. Raw fat can't be filled out (`Hearth.staysIn`) and becomes `RancidFat` when its oldest piece would have rotted (`Hearth.goesOff`, the copper's `offAt`). The five fluids are in `TraditionalPreservationFishMeat_fluids.txt`; their names are in saves, so they never change.

### Charque (0.4.3, 0.4.4)

**Pack Meat in Salt** (`..._Meat.txt`, in hand, no knife) takes the cuts in `F.MEAT_CUTS` (Beef, Steak, Mutton Chop, Venison; pork is left for Country Ham) through fish's `canPack` / `onCreatePacked`, so it costs fish's salt (4 per started 0.5 weight: 4 for any cut). **Salt Packed Meat** and **Salt Cured Meat** carry the cut's food, name ("Steak (Salt Cured)") and icon index (`MEAT_CUTS` order) through `F.carry`. **Cure Salted Meat** (`PressSaltedMeat`) shares the tub and `onPressStart`; a batch of meat takes `F.meatPressHours(heaviest)`: `PRESS_HOURS` at `MEAT_PRESS_WEIGHT` (0.5), by weight, never under `PRESS_MIN_HOURS`. `F.mixedTub` refuses fish and meat in one tub. Cured meat keeps 7 / 14 days, weighs 88-93%, cooks, and takes fish's optional rinse (`F.RINSE_TYPES`).

**Hang Salt Cured Meat to Dry** (`HangMeatToDry`, tags `DryingFrame;Smokehouse`, so the frame, both beams and the Smokehouse) dries it into **Charque** on its own ledger totals: `charque` (open), `charqueCovered`, `charqueSheltered` and `charqueRain` (hours of rain, not snow). The rate is `F.charqueWarmRate` (0 at 0 C, 1 from `CHARQUE_FULL_HEAT` 25 C) × `F.airRate` × `F.charqueWindRate` (0.65 still, 1.2 at `WIND_GOOD`); covered is the same without rain; indoors 0.65 with no wind. An open stack waits while `wetness` > 0, and `F.charqueStep` takes back `CHARQUE_REWET` (a tenth) of its progress per `CHARQUE_RAIN_HOURS` (6) of rain. No minimum rate, no heat damage; spoilage is salt fish's muggy rule (`saltSpoil`). `CHARQUE_HOURS` 120 (sandbox **CharqueDays**). A stack is one kind (`F.hangKind`: salt, cold or meat). Charque keeps 120 / 180, weighs 0.55 of the cured cut (about half the raw), keeps 90% of the food and 70% of the hunger, and is `CantEat`: **Rehydrate Charque** (1 L) gives **Rehydrated Charque** (×1.6 weight, hunger back, thirst 10, 2 / 4 days, `EvolvedRecipeName = Beef`, stand-in `SoakedCharqueIngredient`). Both stages are `salty`, so rinsed meat carries a rinse onto them: a tenth less keeping, two thirds of the thirst. `tools/charque_report.lua` runs the rules on the climate files: covered about 7-11 days June to August, 11-24 in April, weeks in March and October, almost nothing November to February.

### Potted Meat (0.4.5)

Kavurma, confit and *meso u masti* under one English name, in `TraditionalPreservationFishMeatPotted.lua` (items and recipes at the end of `..._Meat.txt`). No station. **Put Meat in Lard or Tallow** (`PutMeatInLard`: an empty Pot or Forged Pot, `Base.Lard` or Tallow, one cut, 1 salt) or **Put Meat in the Melted Fat** (`PutMeatInMeltedFat`: a pot holding 0.5 L `MeltedLard` or `MeltedTallow`, counted as one block) gives a **Pot of Meat in Fat**; **Add Meat to the Pot** (`AddMeatToPot`, `F.canAddPottedCut`) adds cuts one at a time up to `POTTED_MAX` 4, uncooked only. Hand crafts can't take a varying count of items (vanilla's `variable[...]` inputs are station recipes only), hence one cut a recipe. Cuts: `F.POTTED_CUTS`, the Salting Tub's four plus Pork and Pork Chop. The batch lives in the pot's modData `tpfmPotted` (`portions`, `food`, `weight`, `fat` = a full block's food, `blocks` still free): each cut adds its food, two thirds of its weight (`POTTED_WEIGHT`), and a quarter block of fat, or what a part-used block has left. The pot cooks in 120 minutes, burns at 240 (Scrape Out Burnt Fat takes it).

**Pack Potted Meat** (`F.canPackPotted` refuses burnt) puts it in an empty `Base.ClayJar`, `ClayJarGlazed` or `ClayPot`: the pot back and a **Crock of Potted Meat** (`F.fillCrock`): modData `tpfmVessel`, `tpfmPortion` (one portion's food), `tpfmPortionWeight`; food values = portions × portion, `setModelIndex` 0 clay / 1 glazed (IconsForTexture and WorldStaticModelsByIndex), age 0, Cooking XP 3 a cut. It is eaten as Dairy's Jar of Yoghurt: `ISEatFoodAction.complete` / `serverStop` are wrapped, `F.afterCrockUse` relabels it "(N portions)" and sets its weight, or gives the vessel back when under `POTTED_EMPTY`. Keeps by place (0.4.6, `F.keepCrock`): modData `tpfmKeep` = { `base` age, a FluidAgeing `record` of kind `potted` } on its own ledger (global ModData `TraditionalPreservationFishMeatPotted`, `F.recordPotted` every ten minutes); `F.POTTED_KEEPING` = x0.5 at 4 C and below (fridge by the clock, 120 / 180 days), x1 at 15 C (basement: the script's 60 / 90), 60/14 at 25 C and up (14 / 21); a room (Sheltered) reads the season mean, not Weather's season + 10 (`F.pottedTemperature`, own `F.recordPotted` loop); a powered freezer stops it; vanilla Food Spoilage scales it. Looked at hourly on the player, by loot windows, before a transfer and before eating. The first eat (`F.openCrock`, `tpfmOpened`) adds a third of the days left to stale (or to rotten, if stale) once. Sandbox `PottedByPlace`.

### Smokehouse (0.4.7, 0.4.8)

**The chamber** (`entity_Smokehouse_Chamber.txt`): a plank shed two tiles long under one roof slope, the door on the high end. A two-tile entity like vanilla's Drying Rack (`SpriteGridPos`, `ForceSingleItem`; the slots on the engine's master tile). Carpentry 3, 14 Planks, 20 Nails, hammer kept. 6 stacks of up to 15 weight (`Tiles.HANGERS.chamber`, `closed`: nothing hangs in sight). Recipe tag `Smokehouse`: Hang Salt Cured Fish and Hang Salt Cured Meat only, no cold drying; a shed built before the tag gets it when the World first sees it (`CraftLogic.recipeTagQuery` is saved per object). Always **Sheltered** inside (`entry.place`), so it dries as a room does: still air, no rain, gutted fish refused (`F.coldRefusal`). Open air or under an open roof, not in a room (`F.notInRoom`).

**Connect the Firebox** (`TraditionalPreservationFishMeatSmokehouse.lua`, `TraditionalPreservationFishMeatSmokehouseUI.lua`, action `TPFMFlue`): TP Core's Firebox on a tile beside the shed joins it with a clay pipe (2 `ClayPipeSegment` + 1 `Clay`, `MASONS_TROWEL` kept) or a stone trench (3 `FlatStone` + 2 `Clay`, `DIG_GRAVE` kept); `F.FLUE_KITS`, 400 ticks, Masonry XP 5, no skill. On the Firebox's submenu (Core `HearthUI.addFireboxMenu`) and the shed's right-click. Refused (`F.connectRefusal`): the Firebox already joined, the shed already has one, at the door end (`F.chamberSideAllowed`: either long side, or the back tile's gable), the Firebox's own door towards the shed (Core `Hearth.flueSideTo`), or the fire lit or banked. **Disconnect** (200 ticks) gives the pipe segments or flat stones back; the clay is lost. The link lives on the Firebox alone (Core's `flue` and `flueSide` in its Hearth state); the shed finds it by looking at its tiles' neighbours (`F.joinedFirebox`), so there is nothing to break if either goes. Neither picks up while joined (`UI.keepInPlace`). Firebox Info adds the flue's line (`HearthUI.addFireboxNotes`). The rules work per chamber tile (`F.chamberTile`, `F.chamberOther`), ready for a bigger shed. **Joining changes no drying yet**: the shed stays Sheltered.

**Entry art:** where the flue enters, on the long side the camera sees (S facing: east; E facing: south), a pipe stub in a clay collar or the trench's mouth under the sill (hidden behind the Firebox itself). Tiles 860-867 (`Tiles.CHAMBER_ENTRY`, Pipe then Stones, each face, each part; `F.entrySprite`), drawn by the World on either chamber tile (`F.chamberEntry`).

**Doors:** the shed's door tiles are registered with Core's `Doors`: its front tile stays free, and no TP station (Firebox, Hearth, beams, another shed) is built or put down in front of it (`F.notInRoom` asks `Doors.fits`; `UI.keepDoorsFree(Tiles.OUTDOOR)`).

## Seasons

You don't need to watch the forecast. Drying usually takes 12 to 55 days, and the time of year decides how a batch goes.

- **Salt drying** takes 12 days at full speed, which is 20 C or above in dry air. Cold slows it (at 10 C it runs at a quarter speed) and so does air above 50% humidity, but it never drops below about 44% speed, so frost slows it without stopping it.
- **Cold drying** takes 32 days at full speed: 0 to 5 C in a good breeze. Frost slows it (80% at -5 C, 60% below: ice leaves as vapour), so does warmth toward 10 C (67%), and it stops above 10 C. Wind sets the rest: 50% in still air, up to 120% at a wind intensity of 0.25 (`WIND_GOOD`). Live wind in a dry spell averages about 0.11, so a batch hung November to January takes about 50 to 55 days (a gameplay compromise: real stockfish hangs about three months).
- **Fresh fish is fragile**: until a cold stack is 30% dried (`FRESH_UNTIL`), hard frost below -5 C (a tenth of its keeping per 72 hours) and lying wet above 0 C (0.05 per 48 hours) add to its spoilage, scaled by how wet it still is, together never past a quarter (`DAMAGE_CAP`). Rot from a warm season is scaled the same way, so a nearly dry stack caught by an April warm spell loses little and a fresh one rots.
- **Rain**: open stations stop until it ends and the wet has dried off. Progress is paused, not lost. Snow counts as rain for salt fish but doesn't bother cold drying. Rain wets cold fish too, and it waits to dry off (its own wetness, `coldWetness`).
- **Three places.** Core's `Weather.placeOf(station, true)` reads the station's square:
  - **Open**: no roof overhead.
  - **Covered**: a roof overhead but not a room (a porch, an open shed, a floor above with no walls round it). Rain can't reach it. Salt fish dries at the open-air rate in the shade, and the sun can't scorch it. Cold drying runs at 75% (`COVERED_COLD_RATE`): the roof breaks some of the wind, but rain never pauses it and wet never harms it.
  - **Indoors**: a room, either a map building's or a space you built with walls all round and every square roofed (the engine's `isInARoom`). Salt fish runs at 65%, as when fish was dried indoors with the windows open, about 21 to 27 days. Cold drying needs moving air, so a station indoors refuses gutted fish (`F.coldRefusal`; a stack already hanging stays, stopped).

Spoilage goes by the season, not the day: it follows the average temperature of the last 10 days or so, so a warm afternoon in January does nothing.

- **Gutted fish** spoils once that average is above 10 C. It rots on the frame after 2 days at 10 C, sooner the warmer it gets, and the stack comes out rotten straight away.
- **Salt fish** only suffers when the average is above 22 C and the humidity above 40%. Each day of that takes a twentieth of the finished fish's keeping time. In practice that's a few days in a hot July.
- **Hot sun** (above 25 C, `HEAT_ABOVE`) on an open salt stack under half dried (`SALT_FRESH_UNTIL`) hardens the skin over wet flesh and rusts the fat: each 24 hours of it takes 5% (`HEAT_DAMAGE`) off the keeping, scaled by how wet it still is, within the same quarter (`DAMAGE_CAP`). A covered or indoor frame is in the shade. On the forecasts that costs an open stack 2 to 6% from May to August.
- **A warm cure**: while the season averages above 20 C (`WARM_CURE_ABOVE`), fish in the tub sours by the spine before the salt reaches it. It picks up spoilage at a quarter of raw fish's rate (`WARM_CURE_SHARE`), plus as much again for each 10 degrees over: a day at 21 C costs about 14% of its keeping, two days at 25 C about 38%. The cured fish carries that share onto the frame (by weight, for a stack of mixed fish), and the tub's popup shows it while curing. A 2-day cure lost 17% in July and 5% in August here.

Weather is recorded every 10 in-game minutes for the whole world, so frames keep drying while you are away.

## Sandbox options

Under Traditional Preservation (Fish & Meat) in the sandbox options: **Salt drying time** (12 days), **Slowest salt drying** (27 days), **Stockfish drying time** (32 days), **Stockfish maturing time** (60 days), **Salt cure time** (2 days for a batch with a big fish; small fish half) and **Spoilage strictness** (Off, Forgiving, Normal, Harsh). The defaults are set from the season report: stockfish hung from late October to January dries outdoors in about 50-55 days, salt fish in 15-34. Before 0.3.7 stockfish drying followed salt drying at 8/3; it is its own option now. **Butchering gives fat** (on): off, the fat rows are taken out again and Lard is loot only, as in vanilla. **Charque** (on): off, meat can't be packed in salt or hung. **Charque drying time** (5 days in hot sun and a breeze).

## Tuning

Times, capacity, stage values, weather rates and spoilage limits are at the top of `42/media/lua/shared/TraditionalPreservationFishMeat.lua`. `PRESS_HOURS` (the longest press; `PRESS_MIN_HOURS` and the two `PRESS_..._WEIGHT` values set the rest), `DRY_HOURS`, `COLD_DRY_HOURS` and `CHARQUE_HOURS` set the recipe times when the game starts, so they are the only times to edit. Restart the game after changing them. Slot counts are set in the entity scripts and recipes. A station keeps the counts it was built with, even when picked up and placed again.

`tools/build_tileset.py` repaints the stations, hanging fish, icons, the mod list icon and model textures, tints the vanilla fish icons, and writes `42/media/scripts/TraditionalPreservationFishMeat_models.txt` (needs Pillow and the game files). `tools/build_model.py` writes the Stockfish Bale's ground model (`42/media/Models_X/StockfishBale.glb`), its texture and icon (needs Pillow, numpy and the game files). It also writes the generated `42/media/lua/shared/TraditionalPreservationFishMeat/Tiles.lua`, the tile numbers the Lua reads (don't edit it by hand), and `luajit tests/test_tiles.lua` checks the entity scripts against it. `luajit tests/test_curing.lua` checks the curing rules, `luajit tests/test_ui.lua` the station slots and popup, `luajit tests/test_world.lua` the station sprites and the weather record, `luajit tests/test_pallet.lua` the Stockfish Pallet and Bale, and `luajit tests/test_console.lua` the debug commands. The season table above is for the default settings. `luajit tools/season_report.lua` runs the rules against `tools/climate_year.csv`, about a year and a half of forecasts read from the game, so run it after changing any rates; `luajit tools/charque_report.lua` does the same for charque. Hanging charque has its own tiles at the end of the second tileset (716-855: frame, then beam, then gantry; `Tiles.HANGERS.*.meatSprite`), so no station tile moved. `tools/climate_wind.csv` adds a year of the game's forecast wind (one world per start month), which the wind curve (`WIND_GOOD`) was scaled from.

The shared Traditional Preservation core (season averages, station tracking, popups) is the required TP Core mod (`~/MODS/TraditionalPreservationCore`), loaded as `require "TraditionalPreservationCore/Weather"`. Edit it there. The drying and spoilage rates, wetness and the frame's stacks stay in this mod's own files.

`tools/build_meat_icons.py` paints the fat, salted meat, charque and Potted Meat icons from vanilla's loin, steak, mutton, venison, Lard, pot and clay jar icons (needs Pillow and the game files). It is apart from `build_tileset.py`, so the fish art doesn't rebuild with it. The fat rows and their numbers are `F.FAT_ROWS` in `TraditionalPreservationFishMeatFat.lua`; the items and recipes are in `42/media/scripts/TraditionalPreservationFishMeat_Meat.txt`.

## Known prototype caveats

- **Not fully tested in play.** Building, the station windows and sprites have been seen in-game. Seasons, spoilage and the sandbox options haven't been played through.
- A curing batch's hover popup is the mod's own too: the tub's recipe time is the longest cure, so vanilla's
  would count a small batch down from 2 days.
- A hanging stack's hover popup is the mod's own, built from the same weather rules as drying. Its Time Remaining assumes the weather holds. The window's Time Required line above the slots stays at the recipe's base time, and the popup's layout is copied from vanilla, so compare the two after a game update.
- A covered or indoor frame uses the outdoor temperature, so heating the room doesn't speed it up.
- A stack that rots shows its finished sprite until collected.
- Vanilla's cutting can also give a roe sac in season. Gutting and packing only give guts, and gutting a head.
- Vanilla's Divide Food into Bowls only knows vanilla's pots, so the stock has its own two copies of it. Compare them with vanilla's after a game update.
- In a dish's "Contains" list, a fish stage shows one icon for that stage, not its species. Each stage picks its species model when it is made, and the tooltip makes a fresh one every frame, so the list records a stand-in item (`...Ingredient`) instead. Dish names are unaffected.
- Every Fish Head shares one icon, a largemouth bass's head, and lies on the ground as that icon at half size (`ScaleWorldIcon = 0.5`, as the icon art is doubled), as there is no head mesh.
- The season figures come from the game's forecasts for default weather, not from a played year. Sandbox temperature settings will shift them.
- A fish over 4.5 weight has to be gutted and split before it's packed in salt; a fish over 50 weight won't fit on a Fish Gantry, so split it to cold dry.
- The tub holds 35 items at most and a frame stack 25, so fillets under 0.1 weight fill a stack by count before weight.
- A stack's hanging look is picked at random, so it may not match the fish in it. The beams only hang walleye and sauger shapes, as wide fish look wrong at that size.
- On the ground most stages are the vanilla model of that species with a repainted texture, using `WorldStaticModelsByIndex`. Both sides of a fish share one texture, so a gutted fish keeps its head, flecked red, with a pink belly. A half uses the fillet mesh instead, redder, with that species' skin around its edge. Cooked and rotten fish keep their uncooked ground model. Fillets, salmon and fish from other mods use a tinted fillet.
- The tub's and frames' add limits go through the core's `UI.slotRules`, whose add loop is copied from vanilla's `ISCraftLogicPanel.ItemSlotAddItems`, so compare the two after a game update.
- The tiledef number 5129 must be unique among your active mods. A tileset holds 512 tiles at most, and going over drops the whole tile file and every placed station, so the beams are in a second tileset.
- Charque, salted and rehydrated meat lie on the ground as the vanilla model of their cut, untinted.
- Multiplayer is not supported. This mod is singleplayer only for now.
