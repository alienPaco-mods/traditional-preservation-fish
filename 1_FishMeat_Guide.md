## 1. What This Mod Does

You catch more fish than you can eat. This mod turns that surplus into food that keeps for **months (salted)** or **up to two years (cold dried)**, using the two great preserved fish of history: Portuguese *bacalhau* (salt cod) and Norwegian *stockfish* (air-dried in the cold). No power, no fridge, just salt, wood and weather.

Meat follows the same roads: butchering gives fat for **Lard and Tallow** (9¾), and beef, mutton and venison go through the same tub and onto the same frames as **charque**, sun-dried for the summer (9⅞). Any season, any cut including pork can be cooked in that fat and sealed in a clay jar as **Potted Meat** (10).

## 2. Quick Start

> **Requires Traditional Preservation (Core)**, the series' shared mod (TP Core for short). Enable it alongside this one; it loads first.

**Summer catch?** Pack it in salt → cure it in a **Salting Tub** → hang it on a **Fish Drying Frame**.
**Winter catch?** Gut it → hang it on the frame. No salt needed.

| You need | For |
|---|---|
| Sharp knife or meat cleaver (kept) | Packing, gutting, splitting, slicing |
| 4 salt per half weight of fish | Salt route only |
| Carpentry 2, Fishing 2 | Tub and frame |
| 1 L water per fish | Rehydrating to cook |

## 3. The Two Roads: Salt or Cold

| | **Salt route** | **Cold route** |
|---|---|---|
| Season | Any (best in summer) | **Winter only** (about -10 to 5 C) |
| Salt | 4 per half weight of fish | None |
| Keeps | About 4–6 months | About 1–2 years |
| Eating | Straight into soup, stew, rice or pasta (salty) | Must be rehydrated first: too hard to eat |
| Weight | About 37% of the fresh fish | About 18%, the lightest food you can carry |

**Rule of thumb:** salt in summer, cold in winter. The frame takes both kinds at once, in separate stacks.

## 4. Walkthrough: The Salt Route

**Step 1. Pack Fish in Salt** (crafting menu)
Raw fish or fillet + salt by its size + knife (kept) → **Salt Packed** fish.
- Fresh, stale, even **part-eaten** fish works. Rotten, frozen or cooked doesn't.
- **Big fish get split first:** a whole or gutted fish over **4.5 weight** (about 2 kg) is too thick to salt whole. **Gut Fish**, then **Split Fish**, and pack the halves. Bream-sized fish and smaller go in whole, heads on.
- **Salt goes by weight:** 4 for each started half weight of the packed fish, about 15% of its weight. Carry it: only the first 2 can come from a container nearby.

| Fish | Packed weight | Salt |
|---|---|---|
| Bluegill, 0.5 | 0.5 | 4 |
| Walleye, 4.0 | 3.6 | 32 |
| Walleye, 4.5 (the most salted whole) | 4.05 | 36 |
| Catfish, 10.0: too big, gut and split | each half 4.0 | 32 a half |
| Vanilla fillet of that catfish, 0.5 | 4.5 | 36 |

- Any salt works: a vanilla shaker holds **10**, the Salt mod's Evaporated Salt **5** and a Bag of Salt **50**. Part-used salt goes first.

> ✏ *Margin note:* on the Volga a salter reckons 1 to 1.5 kg of salt for 10 kg of vobla; salt cod took up to a third of its weight. The mod charges about 15%. A split catfish costs you more than a whole Bag of Salt, which is why the Salt mod's lick is worth the walk.
- A whole fish over 0.6 weight also gives **Fish Guts** (see Secrets).
- Packed fish keeps only days. Get it in the tub.

**Step 2. Cure in the Salting Tub**
Pick **Cure Salted Fish**, load up to **50 weight / 35 items**, press **Craft**. The board and stones go on for **1 to 2 in-game days**: the **heaviest fish** sets the time, from 1 day (every fish up to 1.1 weight, about half a kilo) to 2 days (any fish of 2.2 weight, about a kilo, or more). Cure small fish together and they come out a day sooner. The tub stays closed until you've taken every **Salt Cured** fish out.
- **Mind a warm spell.** When the last ten days average above 20 C, fish in the tub sours by the spine before the salt reaches it, and the finished fish keeps less long: about a seventh off for a day at 21 C, over a third for two days at 25 C. Hover the batch to see it. Salt in spring and autumn, or cure small fish.

**Optional: rinse off the salt**
Right-click Salt Cured fish and open its submenu: **Rinse Off Salt** is a recipe that takes **0.15 L of clean water** a fish from a bottle or pot you carry (the Craft window does a whole batch). Standing near a sink, bathtub, barrel or well? **Rinse at Sink** in the same submenu rinses every selected fish there. Tainted water won't do. The dried fish comes out **less salty** (a third less thirst, no sour note) but keeps **a tenth less long**: less salt, less protection. Skip it and nothing changes.

> 📜 *On the Volga, vobla makers soak the cured fish for about an hour per day it spent in salt before hanging it in the shade. Too much salt left on the skin crusts over and makes a fish you'll be drinking water after all evening.*

**Step 3. Hang on the Fish Drying Frame**
Pick **Hang Salt Cured Fish to Dry**, load cured fish, press **Craft**. Each press hangs **one stack** of up to 2.5 weight, which dries on its own timer. There are **8 stacks** per frame, so you can keep adding while others dry.

**Step 4. Eat or rehydrate**
**Salt Dried** fish is safe to eat and goes straight into soups and stews. **Rehydrate Fish** (1 L water, container kept) makes a mild fish that cooks like fresh.

> ⚠ Packed and cured fish are **not** safe uncooked. Only drying makes salt fish safe.

## 5. Walkthrough: The Cold Route

1. **Gut Fish**: whole raw fish + knife → **Gutted Fish** (+ guts, and a **Fish Head** from a fish over 1.0 weight). Fillets can't be gutted.
2. **Split Fish** (optional): a gutted fish over 2.5 weight can be split into two **Fish Halves**.
3. **Hang**: pick **Hang Gutted Fish to Cold Dry** on the frame. Frozen fish is fine.
4. **Let it mature**: stockfish ripens in storage over **60 days**, in any container (a fridge slows it, a freezer stops it). The name reads *(Cold Dried, Unripe)* until it's *(Cold Dried, Ripe)*, and the tooltip shows the percentage.
   **Stockfish Pallet** (build menu: hammer, saw, 3 planks, 6 nails, Carpentry 2): stack your stockfish on it the way the warehouses did. In a **loft** (a room upstairs) it ripens **twice as fast** (30 days); in a **ground-floor room**, half as fast again (40 days). Covered, outdoors or down in a damp basement it does no better than a shelf. It holds 20 weight, and the stack on it grows from a third to full as you load it, as long as nothing but stockfish is on it.
   **Stockfish Bale**: select stockfish, right-click, **Tie a Bale**. It takes one use of Twine (or a Rope) and ties up to 10 weight of fish, which then carry 30% lighter. A bale holds nothing but stockfish, and the fish inside keep ripening, on a pallet too. **Untie Bale** gives you the fish and your cord back.

5. **Rehydrate**: Cold Dried fish is as hard as wood: it must be soaked (**Rehydrate Fish**, 1 L water) before you can eat it or cook with it. Ripe stockfish cheers you up (unhappiness -10), and so does the stew it goes into. Unripe, it's dull (+10). Halfway, it's neither.

> ⚠ Cold drying **rots** if the season warms up. Hang from November to mid-February. See the Almanac.

## 8. The Fisherman's Almanac (Seasons)

Drying runs on real weather, recorded every 10 minutes, **even while you're away**.

- **Salt drying**: 12 days at 20 C or warmer in dry air. Cold slows it down, even frost, but never stops it.
- **Cold drying**: 32 days at full speed, between 0 and 5 C with a good wind. Slower in frost (four fifths at -5 C, three fifths below), slower from 5 to 10 C, and it stops above 10 C. **Wind** matters as much: still air halves the speed, a good breeze (the popup's *Good drying wind*) adds a fifth. A Kentucky winter's wind is mostly light, so a batch hung November to January takes about 50 to 55 days. Hang by mid-February at the latest.
- **Fresh fish is fragile.** Until a cold stack is about 30% dried it has no dry skin: hard frost (below -5 C) and lying wet above freezing each take a little off how long the finished fish keeps, a quarter at most. Rot from a warm season also hits a fresh stack hardest and a nearly dry one hardly at all.
- **Rain** pauses open stations until the fish dries off. Progress is **paused, never lost**. Snow pauses salt fish but not cold fish, and cold fish has to dry off after rain too.
- **Where it hangs** (the popup's *Place*):
  - **Open air**: no roof overhead. Walls with no roof are still the open air.
  - **Under an open roof**: a porch, an open shed, a floor laid on the level above. Rain never reaches it. Salt fish dries as fast as in the open, **in the shade**. Cold fish dries at three quarters of the speed, as the roof breaks some of the wind, but never waits out the rain.
  - **Indoors**: inside a room, including one you built yourself with walls all round and a full roof. Salt drying runs at about two thirds of the speed; cold drying **stops**, as the air is still.
- **Hot sun**: above 25 C, salt fish in the open air that is less than half dry takes a little off how long it keeps (the popup says *Too hot in the sun*). A few percent in a Kentucky summer; hang it under an open roof and it's spared.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jan | 28–33 d | 27 d | 27 d | 51–55 d | 60–65 d | 0% | 20% | 0% |
| Feb | 29–33 d | 27 d | 27 d | 52–93 d | 63–101 d | 3% | 16% | 0% |
| Mar | 29–33 d | 26–27 d | 27 d | 74 d | — | 97% | 18% | 0% |
| Apr | 22–30 d | 20–26 d | 24–27 d | — | — | 100% | — | 0% |
| May | 16–25 d | 15–20 d | 22–25 d | — | — | 100% | — | 0% |
| Jun | 16–20 d | 14–17 d | 21–23 d | — | — | 100% | — | 0% |
| Jul | 15–18 d | 14–17 d | 21–23 d | — | — | 100% | — | 17% |

| Sep | 17–34 d | 16–26 d | 23–27 d | — | — | 100% | — | 0% |
| Oct | 30–34 d | 26–27 d | 27 d | 52–57 d | 65–69 d | 30% | 15% | 0% |
| Nov | 29–30 d | 27 d | 27 d | 50–52 d | — | 0% | 10% | 0% |

December batches run past the end of the recorded forecasts, so they have no figure; expect about what January gives. *Keeping lost* is how much of a finished cold batch's keeping time early frost, early wet and a warm spell took, on average. *2-day cure lost* is what a warm cure takes from a batch of big fish cured that month.

## 9. Secrets & Hidden Features

- 🐟 **Fish keep their identity.** A *Small Largemouth Bass* stays one through every stage, so you get *Salt Dried Small Largemouth Bass*. Each fish keeps its own weight and calories, so a batch of mixed sizes comes out mixed too.
- 🎨 **Icons change with the cure.** Pale with salt when cured, browned when salt dried, grey when cold dried, darker again when rehydrated. Gutted fish are drawn **without their heads**, as stockfish were. Halves show red flesh and a backbone.
- 🪝 **Fish Guts are free chum.** Packing or gutting a whole fish over 0.6 weight gives Fish Guts weighing 10% of the fish, the same size limit vanilla uses for filleting. Smaller fish, fillets and salmon give none.
- 🐟 **Fish Heads.** Gutting a fish over 1.0 weight takes its head off, as stockfish makers did: 10% of the weight and 6% of the food go with it. Salted fish keep their heads. A head spoils like fresh fish, makes you sick raw, is a little grim even cooked, and can't be dried. Put it in a soup, a stew or chum, or better, make stock.
- 🍲 **Fish Head Stock.** **Create Fish Head Stock** (Craft menu of the head or the pot, anywhere): a Cooking or Forged Pot with 1.5 L of water + a Fish Head. It's a stew from the start, and the head takes no slot, so you still get **6 stew ingredients** plus seasonings. It starts with the same mood boost as a plain stew (-20 unhappiness), and is named for what you add: *Fish Head Stock with Fish and Carrots*, or plain *Fish Head Stock* past 3 kinds, as vanilla stew falls back to *Stew*. Cook it, then **Divide Food into 4 Bowls** or **2 Bowls** at a table or counter, as with any stew.
- 🔪 **Fillet at any stage.** A whole fish over 0.6 weight can still be cut into 2 fillets after it's been salted, dried or rehydrated, just like a fresh one. The fillets stay at the same stage.
- 🧂 **Changed your mind?** A Gutted Fish or Fish Half can still be packed in salt. It won't give a second lot of guts.
- 🌫 **Damp air matters a little.** Air more humid than 50% slows salt drying, though the game's air is rarely that damp.
- 🔥 **Summer spoilage is gentle.** Salt fish only loses keeping time when the last 10 days averaged over 22 C *and* the air is humid: a few days in a hot July, if any.
- 🎲 **Every frame looks different.** Each stack picks its hanging look at random (whole, headless or split), so a full frame looks hand-hung. Charque hangs as dark cuts, red going to deep brown.
- 📊 **Hover a hanging stack** to see its method, progress, time left, the temperature, how wet it is and how fast it's drying right now, plus a warning if the season is spoiling it.
- 🥄 **Salt Dried fish needs no soaking for soup.** Toss it straight into a pot. Stockfish and charque do: they're too hard to eat as they are.
- 🧺 **One frame, three kinds.** A stack is one kind (salt fish, gutted fish or meat), but a frame can run all three side by side.

## 9½. The Fisherman's Logbook

*Found in a tackle box, the pages stiff with salt and smelling faintly of cod.*

### Food you can carry

Drying takes the water out and leaves the food in. Here's what one unit of inventory weight is worth, made from a fish big enough to give guts and a head:

| Stage | Calories per 1.0 weight | Against fresh |
|---|---|---|
| Fresh fish | about 400 | × 1 |
| Salt Dried | about 975 | × 2½ |
| Cold Dried | about 2,000 | × 5 |
| Rehydrated (cold) | about 670 | back to heavy |
| Fish Head | about 240 | it's mostly bone |

> ✏ *Margin note:* every vanilla fish carries the same calories for its weight, about 400 per 1.0 inventory weight. So a stack of Cold Dried fish weighing 2.5 holds as much food as about 12 weight of fresh fish. Rehydrate it at the pot, not on the road.

> ✏ *Margin note:* a Medium Walleye of 4 weight is about 1,600 calories. Its head takes about 95 of them, which is about right for the cheeks, tongue and gelatin, and why it belongs in the stock pot.

### Why it works the way it does

- **Why does salt dry fish?** Salt draws water out through the flesh (osmosis), and bacteria can't live in what's left. That's why packed fish keeps only days, cured fish a week or two, and fully dried fish for months.
- **Why press it?** The board and stones squeeze out the brine the salt has pulled from the fish, so drying starts from a firmer, drier fish. Norwegian and Portuguese salt cod makers stacked their fish under weights for the same reason.
- **Why does cold drying stop indoors?** Stockfish dries in moving air at around freezing: cold enough that nothing rots, dry and windy enough that the water leaves. In a room the air goes still; under an open roof some wind still gets through.
- **Why dry salt fish in the shade?** In full summer sun the skin dries hard before the inside does, trapping the wet, and the fat turns rusty. Volga salters hang their vobla under awnings and in open sheds.
- **Why do warm stacks rot?** Unsalted fish has nothing protecting it but the cold. Once the season averages above 10 C, it spoils faster than it dries.
- **Why gut it first?** The guts spoil first and fastest. Stockfish makers gutted and headed every fish before it went on the rack.

### Old names, old ways

- **Stockfish** comes from the Lofoten islands of northern Norway, hung on wooden racks called *hjell* from February to May. It was traded across Europe from the Viking age on, and keeps for years.
- **Stockfish was never eaten off the rack.** After about three months on the *hjell*, it was stacked on pallets in dry, airy warehouses for two or three more months to mature, then sorted by hand into a dozen or more grades for Italy, Nigeria and the rest of the trade.
- **Stockfish still travels in bales.** It is pressed and tied into bales of 25 or 50 kg, wrapped in jute, for the trip south.
- **The heads weren't wasted.** Norway still dries its stockfish heads separately, and ships them to West Africa, where they're prized for soups and stews.
- **Bacalhau** (Portuguese) and **klippfisk** (Norwegian) are salt cod, salted, pressed and dried on rocks in the sun. Portugal is said to have a different bacalhau recipe for every day of the year.
- **On the lower Volga** around Astrakhan, large salted fish such as pike are hung from hooks to dry, and smaller ones dried whole with their heads on.

## 9¾. The Butcher's Block: Fat, Lard and Tallow

*The first page of the meat chapters. More follow.*

In the base game a butchered animal gives no fat, and Lard is something you find in a kitchen. Here the fat comes off the carcass, as it does on any farm.

| Animal | Raw fat | On a Butchering Hook | On the ground | Renders to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pig (sow, boar) | Pork Fat | 5 to 7 | 1 | Lard |
| Cattle (cow, bull) | Beef Suet | 8 to 10 | 1 or 2 | Tallow |
| Sheep (ewe, ram) | Mutton Fat | 2 to 4 | 1 | Tallow |

Numbers are for an average animal and an unskilled butcher. A bigger or meatier animal gives more, Butchering skill raises the top end, and roadkill or an old carcass gives less: the game's own butchering rules, the same as for the meat. Piglets, calves, lambs and deer are too lean to give any.

**Rendering, step by step**

1. **Put Pork Fat in a Pot** (or **Put Suet in a Pot**): an empty Cooking Pot or Forged Pot and **2 pieces** of raw fat. Beef Suet and Mutton Fat share a pot, mixed or not.
2. **Cook it** on any stove, campfire or fireplace: about an hour. No station needed.
3. **Pour Off Lard** (or **Pour Off Tallow**): the fat has melted, and your pot now holds half a litre of **Melted Lard** (or **Melted Tallow**).
4. **Strain Lard** (or **Strain Tallow**) through a cloth or a Strainer: one block of fat and one portion of **Cracklings**. The pot and the cloth are kept.

| Strains fat | Notes |
|---|---|
| Cheese Cloth, Dish Towel, Rag, Sheet | Clean and dry. A dirty rag or a wet towel won't do |
| Strainer | The kitchen kind |

Melted fat is a liquid like any other: pour several pots into a bucket and strain it later. It does not spoil.

| Item | Keeps (fresh / rotten) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Pork Fat, Beef Suet, Mutton Fat | 2 / 4 days | Raw, it will make you sick. Fry a piece if you must |
| Lard | does not spoil | The base game's own Lard |
| Tallow | does not spoil | Bakes and seasons wherever Lard does |
| Cracklings | 7 / 14 days | A crisp snack; goes in salads, soups, stews and sandwiches |

> 📝 **Margin note:** a pig on the hook gives 2 or 3 blocks of Lard, about as many calories as its meat. That is close to a real lard pig, and the reason the old breeds were kept.

> 🔥 **Don't walk away.** Left on the heat for two and a half hours the fat burns. A burnt pot gives nothing: **Scrape Out Burnt Fat** gets you the pot back.

**A whole pig at once: the Hearth's copper**

A pot takes two pieces. The copper of a **Hearth** (TP Core) takes the lot.

1. Right-click the Hearth: **Put Fat in the Copper**. Every piece of that kind you carry goes in. Pork fat on its own; suet and mutton fat together.
2. **Light the fire.** Once the copper steams (85 C), the fat needs **one hour** to melt. A banked fire is too cool.
3. **Fill from Copper** into a pot, a bucket or jars: you now carry **Melted Lard** or **Melted Tallow**.
4. **Strain Lard** (or **Strain Tallow**) through a cloth or a Strainer: each half litre gives one block and one Cracklings. The container and the cloth are kept.

| | Pot | Copper |
|---|---|---|
| Pieces at once | 2 | up to 240 |
| Time | 1 hour | heating up, then 1 hour steaming (a pig's fat: about 1 h 45 min) |
| Can it burn? | Yes, if left on the heat 2.5 hours | Never |
| Yield | 2 pieces → 1 block + Cracklings | the same |

Raw fat left in a cold copper goes **rancid** when its oldest piece would have rotted, and is only good for **Empty the Copper**. Melted fat keeps, in the copper or out of it.

> 📝 **Margin note:** the kettle in the yard on hog-killing day had a little water in the bottom, so the fat melted before it could scorch. That is why the copper never burns it.

> 📝 **Margin note:** straining is what makes lard keep. Fat with scraps of meat left in it turns rancid in weeks; strained clean through a flour sack into a crock, it sat in the cellar till the next hog killing. The cloth was boiled and used again.

> 📜 **Old names:** Balkan cooks call cracklings *čvarci* and make them every winter at the pig slaughter. In Britain, tallow lit the house and suet went into the pudding.

## 9⅞. Charque: Sun-Dried Beef

Charque is the summer road for red meat: salted and cured in the **Salting Tub** like fish, then dried in the hot sun on any drying station. It keeps for months.

1. **Pack Meat in Salt** (right-click the meat; no knife needed): a **Beef**, **Steak**, **Mutton Chop** or **Venison** as the butcher gave it, rubbed with **4 salt**. You get **Salt Packed Meat**, named after its cut.
2. Put it in an empty Salting Tub and **Cure Salted Meat**: two days with a Beef or Venison in the batch, about 29 hours for Steaks and Chops alone. The heaviest cut sets the time. A tub salts **fish or meat, never both at once**, and a warm season costs keeping as it does for fish.
3. Out comes **Salt Cured Meat** ("Steak (Salt Cured)"). It keeps one to two weeks and can be cooked as it is, salty. **Rinse Off Salt** works on it as on fish.
4. **Hang Salt Cured Meat to Dry** on the Fish Drying Frame, the Hanging Beam or the Gantry: five days in **hot sun (25 C and up) and a breeze**. Below that it slows, and at freezing it stops.
5. Take down the **Charque** ("Steak (Charque)"): about half the raw weight, 90% of the food, and it keeps **4 to 6 months**.
6. **Rehydrate Charque** (1 L of water) before you eat it: it's salty and as hard as leather. **Rehydrated Charque** is still a little salty, cooks like a steak, goes into stews and pies, and spoils like fresh meat.

| | Salt Packed Meat | Salt Cured Meat | Charque | Rehydrated Charque |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Keeps | 3 / 6 days | 7 / 14 days | **120 / 180 days** (rinsed: 108 / 162) | 2 / 4 days |
| Weight | as the cut | 88 to 93% | about 50% | about 80% |
| Food | 100% | 100% | 90% | 90% |
| Eat it? | No | Cooked; salty and thirsty | **No: rehydrate it** | Cooked; a little salty |

**Weather on the frame**
- **Heat helps.** Full speed from 25 C, two fifths at 10 C, nothing at or below freezing. Unlike salt fish, charque takes no harm from the sun.
- **Wind helps.** Still air dries at two thirds of the speed, a good breeze adds a fifth (the popup shows *Wind*).
- **Rain hurts.** An open frame waits while the meat is wet, and every 6 hours of rain take back **a tenth** of what had dried. Under an open roof it dries as fast as in the open and is never rained on. Indoors, two thirds of the speed.
- **Muggy summers** spoil it a little, under the same rule as salt fish: about a percent in a bad July.

| Month | Open | Under an open roof | Indoors |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jan | 88–119 d | 51–86 d | 65–94 d |
| Mar | 31–59 d | 20–36 d | 25–44 d |
| Apr | 13–34 d | 11–24 d | 14–27 d |
| May | 9–25 d | 7–12 d | 10–16 d |
| Jun | 8–19 d | 7–9 d | 9–12 d |
| Jul | 7–15 d | 7–10 d | 9–12 d |
| Aug | 9–20 d | 7–11 d | 10–13 d |
| Sep | 8–17 d | 7–17 d | 10–26 d |
| Oct | — | 17–65 d | 25–70 d |

From November to February it barely dries at all: that's the season for salt fish and stockfish.

> ✏ **Margin note:** rinse the cured meat before it hangs and the charque keeps a tenth less long, but it rehydrates noticeably less salty (a third less thirst). The charqueadas washed every sheet before the sun.

> 📝 **Margin note:** why no pork? Its fat goes rancid in the sun, so pork has always been salted and then dried cool or smoked. That is the country ham's road, not charque's.

> 📜 **Old names:** in the *charqueadas* of southern Brazil a quarter of beef was opened into thick sheets, *mantas*, salted in brine and then in piles for a few days, washed, and laid out on rails in the sun for four to six days, piled and covered each night. It was never eaten off the rail: cooks soaked it overnight in two or three changes of water first. The word comes from the Andes, where *ch'arki* was llama and alpaca. South Africa's *biltong* is its winter cousin, dried in the cold, dry Highveld air.

## 10. Potted Meat: Meat Under Fat

Potted Meat is the road for any season and any red meat, pork too: cuts cooked slowly in Lard or Tallow with salt, then packed into a clay jar under their own fat. It needs no station, only a pot, a fire and a jar.

1. **Put Meat in Lard or Tallow** (right-click the meat or the fat): an empty **Pot** or Forged Pot, a block of **Lard** or **Tallow**, one cut and **1 Salt**. You get a **Pot of Meat in Fat**.
   Or, straight from rendering: **Put Meat in the Melted Fat** takes the pot of half a litre of **Melted Lard** or **Melted Tallow** that Pour Off leaves (9¾), one cut and Salt. Half a litre is one block.
2. **Add Meat to the Pot**, up to **four cuts** in all: a block of fat covers four. **Beef**, **Steak**, **Mutton Chop**, **Venison**, **Pork** and **Pork Chop** all go in.
3. Cook it on any stove or fire for about **two hours**. It burns if you leave it, and then only **Scrape Out Burnt Fat** gets the pot back. Once it's cooked, no more meat goes in.
4. **Pack Potted Meat** into an empty **Clay Jar**, **Glazed Clay Jar** or **Clay Pot**: the pot comes back, and you have a **Crock of Potted Meat**, one portion for each cut.
5. Eat from it like any food: Eat Quarter, Half or All take portions, and the name counts down ("Crock of Potted Meat (3 portions)"). The **jar or pot comes back** with the last bite.

| | Pot of Meat in Fat | Crock of Potted Meat |
|---|---|---|
| Keeps | 2 / 4 days, as raw meat | **60 / 90 days** |
| Holds | 1 to 4 cuts | a portion a cut |
| Food | the cuts, plus a quarter block of fat for each | the same, cold and ready to eat |
| Eat it? | No: cook it, then pack it | Yes |

**A portion** is the cut's own food plus a quarter of the fat, about 1,000 calories of it. A full crock of four is around **5,000 calories**: few foods carry more. Fewer than four cuts leave the rest of the block behind in the pot, so cook four at a time when you can.

**The fat counts.** A Lard that has been used in cooking only gives what is left of it: a tenth of a block covers one cut with a tenth of the fat, and the next cut gets none.

> ✏ **Margin note:** the glazed jar is the old confit pot. In south-west France the year's duck and goose were kept under their fat in glazed earthenware *pots à confit*, and the plain earthenware jar did the same job for Turkish and Balkan cooks.

> 📝 **Margin note:** why does pork go in here and not on the charque frame? Charque's sun turns pork fat rancid. Under a seal of lard, out of the light and the air, pork has always kept well: the Balkan crock of *meso u masti* is pork in its own lard.

> ⚠ **Margin note:** keep it cold. Farmhouses kept their crocks in cellars through the winter, for months. Modern food safety is stricter: meat sealed under fat is a cold-storage food, kept below about 4 C for no more than three months, under at least a finger's width of fat. Warm, it is a botulism risk, whatever the seal looks like.

> 📜 **Old names:** Turkish *kavurma* and Central Asian *qovurma* are mutton or beef fried slowly in its own fat and the sheep's tail fat until the water is gone, packed into clay pots and sealed, once the winter stores of the army and the steppe. French *confit* means "preserved", from the same Latin as *confection*. In the Balkans it is *meso u masti*, "meat in fat", put down after the autumn pig-killing.

## 11. Tips from the Docks

- **Fillets don't cheat the salt.** A vanilla fillet weighs 5% of its fish but holds half its food, so salted it pays and weighs as half the gutted fish. Fillets cut from a cured fish weigh half of it.
- **Big catch?** A fish heavier than a stack's limit won't fit. Split it in two, or hang it on the Beam or Gantry.
- **An open shed beats a room.** A frame under a porch or lean-to roof dries salt fish at full speed, rain or shine, in the shade. Keep the room for when you have nowhere else.
- **Winter is stockfish season.** Gut everything you catch in December and January. The lightest, longest-keeping food in the game. Turn the heads into stock the same day, as they spoil like fresh fish.
- **Rehydrate only what you'll cook today.** Rehydrated fish keeps just 2 days.
- **Don't eat December's stockfish in January.** Give it the 60 days. A crate in the shed is enough, and the freezer is the one place it won't ripen.
- With the **Salt mod**, a salt lick keeps you packing forever. See the Collection Guide.

## 12. Troubleshooting

| Problem | Why |
|---|---|
| Tub won't accept more | It's full (50 weight or 35 items), curing, or still has cured fish or meat in it; or it holds the other kind (fish or meat) |
| Frame refuses a fish | It takes only Salt Cured fish or meat, or Gutted Fish and halves. Raw, frozen salted, rotten and cooked fish are refused, and a frame stack holds 2.5 weight at most |
| Frame refuses meat | The stack waiting to start is fish: one stack is one kind. Craft it first, then load the meat |
| Cold stack came out rotten | It was hung in a warm season: the last 10 days averaged above 10 C |
| Cold dried fish keeps less than a year | Hard frost or rain caught it in its first days on the frame |
| Not drying | Rain on an open frame (cold fish too, until it dries off); cold drying indoors; or charque at or below freezing |
| Charque lost progress | It was rained on, on an open frame: a tenth for every 6 hours of rain |
| Add Meat to the Pot is greyed out | The pot already has four cuts, or it has cooked: meat goes in raw |
| Pack Potted Meat is greyed out | The pot isn't cooked yet, or it burnt (Scrape Out Burnt Fat). The jar must be empty |
| Put Meat in the Melted Fat is missing | The pot needs a full half litre of Melted Lard or Tallow, as Pour Off leaves it |
| No Eat option on stockfish or charque | They're too hard to eat as they are. Rehydrate them first |
| Salt fish keeps less than it should | A warm cure (the season was above 20 C while it cured) or hot sun on an open frame before it was half dry |
| Time Remaining looks wrong in the window | The window shows the time in the best weather, or the longest cure. Hover the stack or the curing batch for the real time left |
| Pack Fish in Salt won't take a fish | A whole or gutted fish over 4.5 weight must be split first; or you don't carry enough salt for its size |
| No Fish Head from gutting | The fish was 1.0 weight or less, or it was packed in salt (salted fish keep their heads) |
| Create Fish Head Stock missing | The pot needs the full 1.5 L of water |
| Can't divide the stock into bowls | Like vanilla stew, it needs a table or counter nearby, and enough bowls |

## 14. Appendix: The Tester's Toolkit

*For testers, modders and the curious. These commands exist only when the game is started in debug mode (Steam launch option `-debug`). Type them into the in-game **Command Console** and press Enter. `TPFM.help()` lists them in the game.*

> ⚠ They change your save directly, so try them on a test save, not a playthrough you care about.

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `TPFM.weather()` | The drying weather now: temperature, the 10-day season average, humidity, wetness, the wind, the drying speed open, under an open roof, indoors and cold, whether unsalted and salted fish are spoiling, and the running totals. |
| `TPFM.age(hours)` | Gives every hanging stack this many hours of perfect drying weather. `TPFM.age(288)` finishes a salt stack, `TPFM.age(768)` a cold one, `TPFM.age(120)` a charque one. |
| `TPFM.rain(hours)` | Adds hours of rain (default 6) and wets the frames: an open charque stack loses a tenth per 6 hours. Then `TPFM.dry()`. |
| `TPFM.spoil(hours)` | Adds spoiling weather, to test rotting. A fresh cold stack rots after 48. |
| `TPFM.frost(hours)` | Adds hours of hard frost, to test early frost on a fresh cold stack (72 hours take a tenth of its keeping). |
| `TPFM.heat(hours)` | Adds hours of hot sun, to test it on a fresh open salt stack (24 hours take a twentieth of its keeping). |
| `TPFM.warmCure(hours)` | Adds hours of a warm cure at 25 C, to test souring in a running tub (24 hours take about a fifth). |
| `TPFM.dry()` | Clears the wetness, so open and cold frames start drying again at once after rain. |
| `TPFM.fish(name, weight, n)` | Spawns `n` whole fish at that weight, with food scaled to match, e.g. `TPFM.fish("Walleye", 4, 2)`. `TPFM.fish()` lists the names. |
| `TPFM.heads(n)` | Spawns Fish Heads (default food values). |
| `TPFM.stock()` | Spawns a Pot of Fish Head Stock (default values, no head listed). |
| `TPFM.salt(n)` | Spawns vanilla Salt shakers. |
| `TPFM.meat(n)` | Spawns `n` each of Beef, Steak, Mutton Chop and Venison. |
| `TPFM.cured(n)` | Spawns `n` each of those cuts already Salt Cured, ready to hang. |
| `TPFM.fatPot(state)` | Spawns a Pot of Pork Fat, cooked (the default), `"burnt"` or `"raw"`: Pour Off Lard gives the melted fat Potted Meat can use. |
| `TPFM.potted()` | Spawns what Potted Meat takes: a Pot, Lard, Salt, a Steak, Beef, Pork Chop and Pork, and a Clay Jar, Glazed Clay Jar and Clay Pot. |

The Salting Tub runs on the game clock, not the weather, so `TPFM.age` doesn't speed up curing. Use the game's own time controls for that.

**Typical check of the whole salt route:** `TPFM.fish("Walleye", 4, 3)` and `TPFM.salt(10)` (each 4 weight walleye takes 32 salt, and a shaker holds 10), pack and cure, then hang the fish and `TPFM.age(288)`.

**Typical check of charque:** `TPFM.cured(2)`, hang them on a frame, `TPFM.age(120)`, take them down and rehydrate one.

**Typical check of Potted Meat:** `TPFM.potted()`, Put Meat in Lard or Tallow with the Steak, Add Meat to the Pot three times, cook it two hours, Pack Potted Meat into the Glazed Clay Jar, then eat a quarter.

**Typical check of heads:** `TPFM.fish("Walleye", 4)` and `TPFM.fish("Bluegill", 0.9)`, then Gut Fish on both. Only the walleye gives a head.
