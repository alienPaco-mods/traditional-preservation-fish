## 1. What This Mod Does

A good catch or a good kill gives you more than you can eat in a week. This mod turns the surplus into food that keeps for **months or years**, with no power and no fridge: salt, wood, fat, smoke and the weather do the work.

Fish and meat share one workshop. The same **Salting Tub** cures both, the same **drying stations** hang both, the **Smokehouse** smokes both, and butchering gives the **fat** that seals meat away in a clay jar. Which road you take depends on the season and on what's on the table.

| Road | Fish | Meat | Season | Keeps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Salt and dry** | Salt Dried fish (the Volga's way) | **Charque** (beef, mutton, venison) | Any for fish; summer for charque | 4 to 6 months |
| **Freeze-dry in the wind** | **Stockfish** | - | Winter | A year to a year and a half |
| **Hot smoke** | Hot Smoked fish | Hot Smoked meat; pork becomes **Ham** | Any | 1 to 2 weeks, ready to eat |
| **Cold smoke** | Cold Smoked fish | **Suho Meso**, **Mutton Ham**, **Jerked Venison**, smoked pork, **Smoked Country Ham** | Cool months | 4 to 6 months, ready to eat (a ham 6 to 12) |
| **Dry cure in cool air** | - | **Bresaola**, Dry Cured Mutton, Venison and Pork, **Country Ham** | Cool months | The same, with no fire |
| **Under fat** | - | **Potted Meat** (any red meat, pork too) | Any | Weeks to months, by where you keep it |

## 2. Quick Start

> **Requires Traditional Preservation (Core)**, the series' shared mod (TP Core for short). Enable it alongside this one; it loads first.

- **Summer catch?** Pack it in salt → cure it in a **Salting Tub** → hang it on a **Drying Frame**.
- **Winter catch?** Gut it → hang it on the frame. No salt needed.
- **Summer beef?** Pack it in salt → the same tub → the same frame, in the hot sun: **charque**.
- **Butchered a pig?** Render the fat → cook the meat in it → seal it in a clay jar: **Potted Meat**.
- **Want it ready to eat?** Hang salt-cured fish or meat in a **Smokehouse** joined to a **Firebox** and keep the fire stoked for an afternoon: **Hot Smoked**.
- **Want it ready to eat and to keep?** In the cool months, bank the same Firebox to a smoulder for a few hours a day: **Cold Smoked** fish after a day or two of smoke, and smoked dry cured meat such as **Suho Meso** after a week or two of daily fires.
- **Winter beef, mutton or venison?** Country cure it in the tub for ten days, then hang it in the shade for a month or two: **Bresaola** and its kin, or **Suho Meso** with smoke.
- **A leg of pork?** Country cure it in the tub for three weeks in the cold months, then hang it in the Smokehouse or a cellar for the rest of the winter: **Country Ham**.
- **Not sure what to make this month?** Chapter 3 has every road on one page, chapter 15 the calendar, and chapter 16 three worked larders.

| You need | For |
|---|---|
| Sharp knife or meat cleaver (kept) | Packing fish, gutting, splitting, slicing (meat needs no knife) |
| 2 salt per quarter weight of fish; 4 per half-weight cut of meat, 2 per Steak or chop; 10 per leg of pork | Salting |
| Carpentry 2, Fishing 2 | Tub and frame |
| A pot and any fire | Rendering fat, Potted Meat |
| 1 L water | Rehydrating a dried fish or a piece of charque |

## 3. The Larder at a Glance: When, Where, How

Every road on one page. *Start* is when to begin it in Kentucky's weather; *Yield* is what's left of the fresh weight and of its food; *Keeps* is days fresh / days to rotten.

**Fish**

| You make | Start | In the tub | Then where | Takes | Yield | Keeps | Eat it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Salt Dried fish** | Any month; best **May to September** | 1 to 2 days | Any drying station; an open roof is best | 2 to 3 weeks in summer, 4 in winter | 37% weight, 90% food | 120 / 180 | As it is (salty), or in soup |
| **Stockfish** | **November to mid-February** | No salt, no tub | Frame, Beam or Gantry in the open or under an open roof | 7 to 8 weeks, then 60 days maturing | 18% weight, 89% food | **365 / 540** | Soak; then eat it as it is, or cook it (better hot) |
| **Hot Smoked fish** | Any month | 1 to 2 days | Smokehouse, Firebox stoked | 4 to 6 hours of fire | 63% weight, all the food | 7 / 14 | As it is |
| **Cold Smoked fish** | **October to April** (days of 15 C or less) | 1 to 2 days | Smokehouse, Firebox banked | 2 to 4 weeks, with 1 to 2 days of smoke | 37% weight, 90% food | 120 / 180 | As it is |

**Meat**

| You make | Start | In the tub | Then where | Takes | Yield | Keeps | Eat it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Charque** (beef, mutton, venison) | **May to September** | Corned, 1 to 2 days | Any drying station in the sun or under an open roof | 1 to 3 weeks | 50% weight, 90% food | 120 / 180 | Soak, then cook |
| **Hot Smoked meat**; pork becomes **Ham** | Any month | Corned, 1 to 2 days | Smokehouse, Firebox stoked | 6 to 8 hours of fire | 70% weight, all the food | 7 / 14 (Ham 5 / 10) | As it is |
| **Dry cured cuts**: Bresaola, Suho Meso and their kin | **October to April** | Country Cured, 10 days | Smokehouse, any station under a roof, or a Frame or Beam in a room or basement | 3 to 12 weeks | 50% weight, 90% food | 120 / 180 | As it is |
| **Country Ham** (a Leg of Pork) | **October to March** | Country Cured, 21 days | The same | 6 weeks to 5 months | 50% weight, 90% food | **180 / 365** | As it is |
| **Potted Meat** (any cut, pork too) | Any month; whenever you butcher | None | A pot, a fire, a clay jar | 2 hours | The meat and its fat | 14 to 120, by where it's kept | As it is |
| **Lard, Tallow** | Whenever you butcher | None | A pot, or the Hearth's copper | 1 hour | 2 pieces of fat a block | Don't spoil | For cooking and potting |

**What each road costs**

| | Salt | Fuel | Built first |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salt Dried fish | **4 per half weight**: 160 for a full frame | None | Tub, a drying station (Carpentry 2) |
| Stockfish | **None** | None | A drying station |
| Charque, dry cured meat | **4 a cut**, 10 a leg: 200 for a cow | None; a little wood for smoke | Tub, a drying station, or the Smokehouse |
| Hot and cold smoking | As above | Wood, hours of it | Smokehouse (Carpentry 3) and Firebox (Masonry 2) |
| Potted Meat | **1 a pot** | A cooking fire | A pot and a clay jar |

**Rule of thumb:** salt fish in summer, stockfish in winter, charque in the hot months, dry cured meat and ham from the autumn slaughter, Potted Meat whenever you butcher, and hot smoke when you want something good to eat this week.

## 4. At the Cleaning Table: Fish, Heads and Fat

### Fish

| Craft | Takes | Gives |
|---|---|---|
| **Gut Fish** | A raw whole fish, knife or cleaver (kept) | **Gutted Fish**, plus **Fish Guts** (fish over 0.6 weight) and a **Fish Head** (over 1.0) |
| **Split Fish** | A Gutted Fish over 2.5 weight, knife | Two **Fish Halves**, each half the weight and food |
| **Slice Fish** | A whole fish over 0.6 weight at any salted, dried or rehydrated stage, knife | Two fillets at the same stage |

- **Fillets can't be gutted**: they go to the salt.
- **Gutting takes the head off**, as stockfish makers did. A fish of 1.0 weight or less keeps its head.
- **Fish Guts** weigh 10% of the fish and go in chum. Smaller fish, fillets and salmon give none.
- **A Fish Head** weighs 10% of the fish and takes 6% of its food with it. It spoils like fresh fish (3 / 6 days), makes you sick raw, is a little grim even cooked, and can't be dried. It goes in a soup, a stew, chum, or best of all a stock.

**Fish Head Stock.** **Create Fish Head Stock** (Craft menu of the head or the pot, anywhere): a Cooking Pot or Forged Pot with **1.5 L of water** + a Fish Head. It's a stew from the start and the head takes no slot, so you still get **6 stew ingredients** plus seasonings. It starts with a plain stew's mood boost (-20 unhappiness) and is named for what you add: *Fish Head Stock with Fish and Carrots*, or plain *Fish Head Stock* past 3 kinds. Cook it, then **Divide Food into 4 Bowls** or **2 Bowls** at a table or counter. Keeps 3 / 5 days.

### Meat

Meat goes to the salt or the pot as the butcher cuts it: **no knife work needed**. The cuts are the base game's: Beef and Venison (0.5 weight), Steak and Mutton Chop (0.3), Pork (0.5) and Pork Chop (0.3).

**The Leg of Pork.** A grown pig also gives some of its Pork as whole hind legs, **2.5 weight** each, for country ham. They aren't extra meat: five of the Pork the pig gave become one leg, with their food, so the pig is worth what it always was.

| Pig | On a Butchering Hook | On the ground |
|---|---|---|
| An average sow or boar | 1 or 2 legs | usually none |
| A big, meaty one | 2 or 3 legs | 1 at most |
| A piglet | none | none |

At most half a pig's Pork goes into legs, and never more than three. Turn it off in the sandbox (*Butchering gives legs of pork*) and the Pork stays Pork.

> *Margin note:* why three, when a pig has two hind legs? The front legs cure too: Kentucky farms hung the "picnic" shoulders beside the hams, and sold them a little cheaper.

### Fat

In the base game a butchered animal gives no fat, and Lard is something you find in a kitchen. Here the fat comes off the carcass, as on any farm.

| Animal | Raw fat | On a Butchering Hook | On the ground | Renders to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pig (sow, boar) | Pork Fat | 5 to 7 | 1 | Lard |
| Cattle (cow, bull) | Beef Suet | 8 to 10 | 1 or 2 | Tallow |
| Sheep (ewe, ram) | Mutton Fat | 2 to 4 | 1 | Tallow |

Numbers are for an average animal and an unskilled butcher. A bigger or meatier animal gives more, Butchering skill raises the top end, and roadkill or an old carcass gives less: the game's own butchering rules, as for the meat. Piglets, calves, lambs and deer are too lean to give any. Raw fat keeps 2 / 4 days. What to do with it: chapter 12.

## 5. The Salting Tub

**Build:** Carpentry 2, Fishing 2: 4 planks, 6 nails, 2 twine; hammer and saw kept. Holds **50 weight or 35 items**, **fish or meat, never both at once**.

### Step 1. Pack in salt (crafting menu)

| | **Pack Fish in Salt** | **Pack Meat in Salt** |
|---|---|---|
| Takes | A raw fish, fillet, Gutted Fish or Fish Half; knife or cleaver (kept) | A Beef, Steak, Mutton Chop, Venison, Pork, Pork Chop or Leg of Pork; no knife |
| Salt | **2 for each quarter weight** of the packed fish, to the nearest quarter, never under 2 (about 15%) | The same: **4** for a Beef, Venison or Pork, **2** for a Steak or a chop; a Leg of Pork **half the rate**, **10** for 2.5 weight (about 8%) |
| Gives | **Salt Packed** fish (+ Fish Guts from a whole fish over 0.6) | Salt Packed meat, named for its cut (*Steak (Salt Packed)*) |
| Not | Rotten, frozen or cooked fish | Rotten, frozen or cooked meat |

- Fresh, stale, even **part-eaten** fish works.
- **Big fish get split first:** a whole or gutted fish over **4.5 weight** (about 2 kg) is too thick to salt whole. **Gut Fish**, then **Split Fish**, and pack the halves. Bream-sized fish and smaller go in whole, heads on.
- **Salt is carried:** only the first 2 can come from a container nearby. Any salt works: a vanilla shaker holds **10**, the Salt mod's Evaporated Salt **5** and a Bag of Salt **50**. Part-used salt goes first.
- Packed fish and meat keep only 3 / 6 days. Get them in the tub.

| Fish | Salting weight (what the salt is charged on) | Salt |
|---|---|---|
| Bluegill, 0.3 | 0.3 | 2 |
| Bluegill, 0.5 | 0.5 | 4 |
| Walleye, 4.0 | 3.6 | 28 |
| Walleye, 4.5 (the most salted whole) | 4.05 | 32 |
| Catfish, 10.0: too big, gut and split | each half 4.0 | 32 a half |
| Vanilla fillet of that catfish, 0.5 on the scales | 4.5 (see below) | 36 |

The salting weight is the gutted fish's own for a whole fish or a half. A vanilla fillet is the exception: the game weighs it at a twentieth of its fish though it holds half the food, so the salt goes by what it holds, half the gutted fish, and it comes out of the tub that heavy.

> *Margin note:* on the Volga a salter reckons 1 to 1.5 kg of salt for 10 kg of vobla, 10 to 15%. The mod charges about 15%. A split catfish costs you more than a whole Bag of Salt, which is why the Salt mod's lick is worth the walk.

### Step 2. Cure

Pick the cure, load the tub, press **Craft**. Until then the tub stands open and shows its top layer in the salt: fish, red cuts, pale pork, or a leg (a leg always shows, it fills the tub). The board and stones go on, and the **heaviest piece sets the time**. Fish has one cure; meat has two:

| Recipe | Gives | For |
|---|---|---|
| **Cure Salted Fish** | **Salt Cured** fish (*Walleye (Salt Cured)*) | Every fish road |
| **Corn Salted Meat** (the short cure) | **Corned** meat (*Steak (Corned)*) | Charque and hot smoking. Not a Leg of Pork: too thick |
| **Country Cure Salted Meat** (the long cure) | **Country Cured** meat (*Leg of Pork (Country Cured)*) | The Smokehouse's dry cured meat and country ham |


| Batch | Cure |
|---|---|
| Fish all up to 1.1 weight (about half a kilo) | **1 day** |
| Any fish of 2.2 weight (about a kilo) or more | **2 days** |
| Corned: Steaks and Chops alone | about **29 hours** |
| Corned: any Beef, Venison or Pork | **2 days** |
| Country Cured: cuts | **10 days** |
| Country Cured: any Leg of Pork in the batch | **21 days** |

The clock runs while you're away. The tub stays closed until you've taken every piece out.

- **Mind a warm spell.** When the last ten days average above 20 C, the flesh by the spine starts to spoil (bone taint) before the salt reaches it, and the finished batch keeps less long: about a seventh off for a day at 21 C, over a third for two days at 25 C. Hover the batch to see it (*Spoiling (Warm cure)*). Salt in spring and autumn, or cure small fish. **The long cure wants the cold**: its salt takes weeks to reach the bone, so it is safe only while the last ten days average **10 C or less**, about **October to April**. Warmer, it spoils in step: at 12 C a leg loses two fifths of its keeping and cuts a fifth; at 15 C a leg rots in the tub and cuts lose half. The short cure and fish only mind 20 C.
- **Salt Cured** fish, **Corned** and **Country Cured** meat keep 7 / 14 days and are raw: cook them before eating, or hang them.

> *Corned* is the old word for dry-salted with coarse grains of salt, "corns": corned beef was a short cure, boiled with cabbage, long before it came in tins. *Country cured* is what Kentucky still calls a ham rubbed in dry salt and left in the box: "a day and a half to the pound", hogs killed after the first hard frost so the meat cools before the salt reaches the bone.

### Optional: rinse off the salt

Right-click Salt Cured fish or Corned or Country Cured meat and open its submenu: **Rinse Off Salt** takes **0.15 L of clean water** a piece from a bottle or pot you carry (the Craft window does a whole batch, even in the dark). Near a sink, bathtub, barrel or well? **Rinse at Sink** in the same submenu rinses every selected piece there. Tainted water won't do.

Rinsed, whatever it becomes is **less salty** (a third less thirst; Salt Dried fish also loses its sharp tang) and keeps **a tenth less long**. Skip it and nothing changes.

> *On the Volga, vobla makers soak the cured fish about an hour for each day it spent in salt before hanging it in the shade. The charqueadas of Brazil washed every sheet of beef before it went out in the sun.*

## 6. The Drying Stations

Every drying station works the same way: pick a hanging recipe, load it, press **Craft**. Each press hangs **one stack**, which dries on its own timer, so you can keep adding stacks while others dry. A stack is one kind (salt fish, gutted fish or meat); a station runs all kinds side by side.

| Station | Skill | Materials (hammer kept) | Holds | Where |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Drying Frame** | Carpentry 2, Fishing 2 | 6 long sticks, 3 nails, 4 twine | 8 stacks × 2.5 weight, 25 pieces | Anywhere, a room too |
| **Hanging Beam** | Carpentry 4, Fishing 2 | 6 planks, 6 nails, 1 large hook | 4 stacks × 15 weight, 12 pieces | Anywhere: open air, under a roof, in a room or a basement |
| **Gantry** | Carpentry 6, Fishing 2 | 2 logs, 2 planks, 8 nails, 2 large hooks | 2 stacks × 50 weight, 6 pieces | Open air or under an open roof |
| **Smokehouse** | Carpentry 3 | 14 planks, 20 nails | 6 stacks × 15 weight, 25 pieces | Open air or under an open roof; two tiles; chapter 10 |

| Recipe | Frame, Beam, Gantry | Smokehouse |
|---|---|---|
| **Hang Salt Cured Fish to Dry** | Yes | Yes |
| **Hang Salted Meat to Dry**: Corned beef, mutton, venison (charque) | Yes, in the sun or under an open roof | Yes: a hot cook, never charque |
| **Hang Salted Meat to Dry**: Country Cured meat, pork too (dry curing) | Yes, **in the shade only**: under a roof, in a room or a basement | Yes |
| **Hang Salted Meat to Dry**: Corned pork | No | Yes: a hot cook |
| **Hang Gutted Fish to Cold Dry** (stockfish) | Yes, never in a room | **No** |

**Where it hangs** (the stack's *Place*):
- **Open air**: no roof overhead. Walls with no roof are still the open air. Rain pauses drying until the wet has dried off.
- **Under an open roof**: a porch, an open shed, a floor laid on the level above. Rain never reaches it, and it's **in the shade**.
- **Indoors**: inside a room, including one you built yourself with walls all round and a full roof, and always inside the Smokehouse. Still air: salt fish dries at about **two thirds** of the speed, and **gutted fish is refused** (stockfish needs moving air). **Meat indoors is never charque**: it dry cures (chapter 11).
- **Basement**: any level below ground. Indoors, but at the **cellar's own temperature**, not the day's: about 5 C from January to March, 13 C from July to September, a month behind the season. Whatever dries there goes by it, so salt fish crawls (about **27 days** a batch, all year): a cellar is for keeping salt fish, not drying it.

**Hover a stack** to see its method, progress, time left, place, the temperature, the wind, how wet it is and how fast it's drying right now, a warning if the season is spoiling it, and in a Smokehouse its smoke.

**The station's own window tells you the rest.** Under *In Progress*, *Done* and *Take All*, every station (the Salting Tub and the Smokehouse too) lists the place, how many stacks are in use, the air now and over the season (below ground, the basement's own temperature), and a bar for every running batch with its share, its time left or the reason it stands still. Under the bars it lists what wants your eye: a batch that is spoiling, a hot cook under way, the smoke so far, pieces loaded but never started, finished ones waiting to be taken.

> *Margin note:* the times left assume today's weather holds. Nobody on the Volga could promise that either.

## 7. Salt Dried Fish

1. **Pack** and **cure** (chapter 5), rinse if you like.
2. **Hang Salt Cured Fish to Dry** on any drying station. **12 days** at best: 20 C or warmer in dry air. Cold slows it, even frost, but never stops it.
3. Take down **Salt Dried** fish: about 37% of the fresh fish's weight, 90% of its food, keeps **120 / 180 days** (rinsed 108 / 162).
4. **Eat it** as it is (very salty, and safe), toss it straight into soup, stew, rice or pasta, or **Soak Fish** (1 L of water, container kept) for a mild fish that cooks like fresh and keeps 2 / 4 days.

- **Hot sun**: above 25 C, salt fish in the open air that is less than half dry takes a little off how long it keeps (the popup says *Too hot in the sun*). A few percent in a Kentucky summer; under an open roof it's spared.
- **Muggy summers**: salt fish only loses keeping time when the last 10 days averaged over 22 C *and* the air is humid: a few days in a hot July, if any.

> **Warning:** Packed and cured fish are **not** safe uncooked. Drying is what makes salt fish safe.

## 8. Charque

Charque is the summer road for red meat: salted and cured in the tub like fish, then dried in summer heat. Hot sun is best, and an open roof does as well and keeps the rain off; what it can't do without is the warmth.

1. **Pack Meat in Salt** and **Corn Salted Meat** (chapter 5). Out comes **Corned** meat (*Steak (Corned)*). Beef, mutton and venison only: pork is never charque, and Country Cured meat is for dry curing (chapter 11).
2. **Hang Salted Meat to Dry** on any drying station **in the open or under an open roof**: **five days in hot sun (25 C and up) and a breeze**.
3. Take down the **Charque** (*Beef Charque*, *Mutton Charque*, *Venison Charque*): about half the raw weight, 90% of the food, keeps **120 / 180 days** (rinsed 108 / 162).
4. **Soak Charque** (1 L of water) before you eat it: it's salty and as hard as leather. Soaked (*Beef Charque (Soaked)*) it's still a little salty, cooks like a steak in about 40 minutes, goes into soups, stews, pies and more, and spoils like fresh meat (2 / 4 days).

**Weather on the frame**
- **Heat helps.** Full speed from 25 C, two fifths at 10 C, nothing at or below freezing. Unlike salt fish, charque takes no harm from the sun.
- **Wind helps.** Still air dries at two thirds of the speed, a good breeze adds a fifth.
- **Rain hurts.** An open station waits while the meat is wet, and every 6 hours of rain take back **a tenth** of what had dried. Under an open roof it dries as fast as in the open and is never rained on. **Not indoors**: charque is the sun's and the wind's. Corned meat hung in a room or a basement dry cures as in the Smokehouse, **under-salted**, and spoils as it hangs.
- **Muggy summers** spoil it a little, under salt fish's rule.

| Month | Open | Under an open roof |
|---|---|---|
| Jan | 88–119 d | 51–86 d |
| Mar | 31–59 d | 20–36 d |
| Apr | 13–34 d | 11–24 d |
| May | 9–25 d | 7–12 d |
| Jun | 8–19 d | 7–9 d |
| Jul | 7–15 d | 7–10 d |
| Aug | 9–20 d | 7–11 d |
| Sep | 8–17 d | 7–17 d |
| Oct | — | 17–65 d |

From November to February it barely dries at all: that's the season for stockfish, Potted Meat and dry curing.

> **Margin note:** why no pork charque? Its fat goes rancid in sun and wind, so pork has always been salted and then kept cool, smoked or put down in lard. Corned pork goes to the Smokehouse for a hot cook (chapter 10), Country Cured pork hangs in the shade (chapter 11), and Potted Meat takes it any day.

> **Old names:** in the *charqueadas* of southern Brazil a quarter of beef was opened into thick sheets, *mantas*, salted in brine and then in piles for a few days, washed, and laid out on rails in the sun for four to six days, piled and covered each night. It was never eaten off the rail: cooks soaked it overnight in two or three changes of water first. The word comes from the Andes, where *ch'arki* was llama and alpaca.

## 9. Stockfish

Stockfish is unsalted fish dried by frost and wind. It needs no salt and no tub, keeps the longest and weighs the least, but only works in a cold season.

1. **Gut Fish** (chapter 4). **Split Fish** if it's over 2.5 weight, or hang it whole on a Beam or Gantry.
2. **Hang Gutted Fish to Cold Dry** on a frame, Beam or Gantry, **in the open or under an open roof**. Frozen fish is fine.
3. **32 days** at best: between 0 and 5 C with a good wind. A Kentucky winter's wind is mostly light, so a batch hung November to January takes about 50 to 55 days. **Hang by mid-February at the latest.**
4. Take down the **Stockfish** (*Walleye Stockfish*): about 18% of the fresh weight, 89% of the food, keeps **365 / 540 days**: enough to reach next winter's fish.
5. **Let it mature**: stockfish matures in storage over **60 days**, in any container (a fridge slows it, a freezer stops it). Then the name reads *Walleye Stockfish (Matured)*; until then the tooltip shows how far along it is.
6. **Soak Fish** (1 L of water) before you eat or cook it (*Walleye Stockfish (Soaked)*): it's as hard as wood. Matured stockfish cheers you up (unhappiness -10), and so does the stew it goes into; fresh off the rack it's dull (+10); halfway it's neither. Soaked, it keeps 2 / 4 days.

**Weather**
- Slower in frost (four fifths at -5 C, three fifths below), slower from 5 to 10 C, and it **stops above 10 C**.
- **Wind** matters as much: still air halves the speed, a good breeze (the popup's *Good drying wind*) adds a fifth. Under an open roof, three quarters of the speed, but it never waits out the rain.
- **Fresh fish is fragile.** Until a stack is about 30% dried it has no dry skin: hard frost (below -5 C) and lying wet above freezing each take a little off how long the finished fish keeps, a quarter at most.
- **Warm seasons rot it.** Once the last 10 days average above 10 C, a fresh stack spoils faster than it dries; a nearly dry one hardly suffers.

**Storing it**
- **Stockfish Pallet** (build menu: Carpentry 2; 3 planks, 6 nails; hammer and saw kept): stack your stockfish on it the way the warehouses did. In a **loft** (a room upstairs) it matures **twice as fast** (30 days); in a **ground-floor room**, half as fast again (40 days). Covered, outdoors or in a damp basement it does no better than a shelf. It holds 20 weight; the stack on it grows from a third to full as you load it, as long as nothing but stockfish is on it. Right-click it to see its speed there.
- **Stockfish Bale**: select stockfish, right-click, **Tie a Bale**. It takes one use of Twine (or a Rope) and ties up to 10 weight of fish, which then carry 30% lighter. A bale holds nothing but stockfish, and the fish inside keep maturing, on a pallet too. **Untie Bale** gives you the fish and your cord back.

> **Warning:** Cold drying **rots** if the season warms up. Hang from November to mid-February. See the Almanac.

## 10. The Smokehouse

A plank shed two tiles long under one sloping roof, the door on the high end with a smoke slot under the eave. On its own it's a dry room for salt fish and the place where meat **dry cures**; with a **Firebox** joined to it, it **hot smokes** on a stoked fire and **cold smokes** on a banked one.

| | |
|---|---|
| Build | Carpentry 3: **14 planks, 20 nails**, hammer kept (Build menu, Furniture) |
| Holds | **6 stacks** of up to **15 weight**, 25 pieces |
| Takes | **Hang Salt Cured Fish** and **Hang Salted Meat** only; the one place Corned pork may hang |
| Inside | always **Indoors**: no rain, no wind, wherever the shed stands |
| Stands | in the open or under an open roof, not inside a room; the tile in front of its door stays clear |

1. **Build it** with the door facing where you'll walk up to it. Nothing can be built on the tile in front of the door afterwards.
2. **Click either tile**: one window, **Smokehouse**, with the hanging recipes. Load it as you would a frame. Nothing hangs in sight: hover a stack in the window to see how it's doing.
3. **With no fire it dries like a room**: salt fish at about **two thirds** of its open speed, never rained on. **Meat dry cures** here on its own slow clock (chapter 11), and never comes out Charque.
4. **No stockfish.** Gutted fish needs moving air, so the shed doesn't offer cold drying.
5. **Moving it:** empty and unjoined, it picks up as one item and puts down again as both tiles.

### Joining a Firebox

The **Firebox** comes from *Traditional Preservation (Core)* (Masonry 2: 8 Clay Bricks, 2 Clay; trowel kept; see the Core Guide). Build one on a tile beside the shed, then lay its flue into the wall.

| Way | Takes | Tool kept | Comes back on Disconnect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **With a Clay Pipe** | 2 Clay Pipe Segments, 1 Clay | Mason's Trowel | 2 Clay Pipe Segments |
| **With a Stone Trench** | 3 Flat Stones, 2 Clay | Shovel, Spade or Entrenching Tool | 3 Flat Stones |

- **Connect the Firebox** is on the Firebox's own menu and on the shed's right-click. No skill; about half an hour of work and a little Masonry experience. The clay is lost when you take the flue up.
- **Where it may stand:** beside either long side of the shed, or behind its back end. Not at the door end, and **not with the Firebox's own door towards the shed**: its flue leaves from its back or a flank.
- **One Firebox to a shed**, and the fire must be **out** (not lit, not banked) to connect or disconnect.
- **Joined, neither moves** until you **Disconnect the Firebox**.
- **What you see:** the clay pipe climbs from the Firebox's top and runs into the wall through a collar of clay; a trench runs from its foot under flat stones. **Firebox Info** says where its flue goes.

### Hot smoking

Light the joined Firebox and keep it going. A lit Firebox is **stoked**; **Bank the Fire** (Core) rakes it down to a smoulder, **Stoke the Fire** brings it back up.

- Every salt stack in the shed counts the **stoked hours in a row**. When a stack has enough, it **finishes at once** as **Hot Smoked Fish** or **Hot Smoked** meat (*Beef (Hot Smoked)*); **pork comes out as a Ham**, the game's own, with the cut's weight and food, so **Slice Ham** and every ham dish take it. And a **smoked ham hangs by the shed's door** until you take it out.
- **How long** goes by the **heaviest piece** in the stack:

| Stack | Stoked hours in a row |
|---|---|
| Salt Cured fish, all up to 1.1 weight | **4** |
| Salt Cured fish of 2.2 weight or more | **6** (in between by weight) |
| Steaks and Mutton Chops (0.3) | **6** |
| Any Beef, Venison or Pork (0.5) | **8** |
| A stack with a **Leg of Pork** in it | **never**: a leg takes no hot cook, its stoked hours are smoke |

- **Watch the stack.** While the run is counting, the stack's yellow bar follows the cook and a small **Hot Smoked icon** sits in its bottom left corner, where a salt stack shows its salt. A full bar is a finished cook. If the run breaks, the icon goes and the bar drops back to how far the stack has dried.
- **Keep it going.** Adding wood, or banking it **for up to an hour**, doesn't break the run (the banked time doesn't count towards it either). Let it go out or banked for longer and the run is over: those hours count as **smoke** instead (the popup's *Smoke* line), and the next stoking starts from nothing.
- **Away from home?** The fire burns its wood while you're gone: a fire with three hours of wood gives three hours, however long you stay away.
- **Short of a cook, fish loses nothing.** The stack keeps drying as the shed always does, and comes out Salt Dried or Cold Smoked when it's dry.
- **Corned meat must get its cook.** The short cure salts meat for days, not weeks: left to dry in the shed it is **under-salted** and spoils (chapter 11). Meat for the long hang takes the long cure.
- **Want Salt Dried fish instead?** Hang it with the fire out, or on a frame. **Charque** comes from the frames only.
- The stack's popup shows **Hot Smoke: x of y h stoked in a row**, and the **Smoke** line for cold smoking (below).

| | Hot Smoked Fish | Hot Smoked meat |
|---|---|---|
| Weight | about 63% of the fresh fish | about 70% of the raw cut |
| Food | 100% | 100% |
| Keeps | **7 / 14 days** | **7 / 14 days** |
| Eat it | **As it is**: cooked, a little salty, a small mood lift | **As it is**: cooked, a little salty, a small mood lift |
| Cook with it | Soups, stews, rice, pasta, sandwiches, salads | Soups, stews, pies, rice, pasta, sandwiches, salads, tacos, burritos |

> **Margin note:** a hot smoked fish is cooked through: it has to reach about 63 C inside and stay there, in one run. That's why the hours must come in a row. Stoke an hour a day for a week and you get smoke on a drying fish, not a cooked one.

> **Old names:** the Volga sells its fish three ways from the smokehouse: *goryachego kopcheniya*, hot smoked, golden and soft and eaten within days; *kholodnogo kopcheniya*, cold smoked over days and keeping for months; and *kopchyono-vyalenaya*, smoked a while and then air-dried, which is what a broken run gives you here. Every Kentucky farm had its smokehouse, hickory smoke and a ham hanging by the door, and the low firebox outside the wall is how many a Bosnian *pušnica* is fed: the fire stays outside where it can be tended, and only its smoke goes in.

> **Margin note:** why may nothing stand in front of a door? The same rule holds for the Hearth's open mouth and the Firebox's door: you have to be able to reach the fire and the hooks.

### Cold smoking

**Bank** the joined Firebox and let it smoulder. The stacks dry as they always do, in smoky air, and the smoke is counted.

- **Smoke** = the hours the Firebox smouldered, plus any stoked run that broke off before it cooked the stack. Only hours on a day of **15 C or less** count, whichever way the fire burned: on a warmer day the smoulder heats the meat past 30 C, which cooks it rather than cures it.
- **When the stack is dry**, it looks at its smoke. With its **hours of smoke** it comes out **Cold Smoked**; short of them, fish is plain Salt Dried and meat is **air dried** (Bresaola and its kin, chapter 11). The hours go by the **heaviest piece**, and needn't come in a row:

| Stack | Hours of smoke |
|---|---|
| Salt Cured fish, all up to 1.1 weight | **24** |
| Salt Cured fish of 2.2 weight or more | **48** (in between by weight) |
| Steaks, chops and any Beef, Venison or Pork | **60** |
| A stack with a **Leg of Pork** | **120** (in between by weight) |

- A banked fire for eight hours a day gives a fish stack its smoke in three to six days, a stack of cuts in about a week, a leg in about two.
- **A fire warms the shed, and warm air dries.** Any hour with the Firebox burning, the stacks dry as if the shed were **15 C warmer** than outdoors when banked, **20 C warmer** when stoked, and the warmed air is drier too. So the smoulder speeds drying even in the cold months, from a little under freezing upward. Meat gains as much. The popup's **Drying Rate** shows the rate while the fire burns.

| Salt fish, damp day | Fire out | Banked | Stoked |
|---|---|---|---|
| -5 C | 27 days | 27 days | 21 days |
| 0 C | 27 days | 21 days | 12 days |
| 2 C | 27 days | 17 days | 12 days |
| 5 C and up | 27 days | 12 days | 12 days |

> *Days as if the fire burned every hour; a few hours a day lands between the two columns.*
- The popup's **Smoke** line reads *x of y h of smoke*. Over 15 C it says **Too warm to count**.

| | Cold Smoked Fish |
|---|---|
| Weight | about 37% of the fresh fish, as Salt Dried |
| Food | 90% |
| Keeps | **120 / 180 days** (rinsed 108 / 162) |
| Eat it | **As it is**, sliced thin: a little salty, a small mood lift |
| Cook with it | Soups, stews, rice, pasta, sandwiches, salads |

**Meat in the smoke** takes the same fire and the same count, but it hangs for weeks, not days, and it has a chapter of its own: **Dry Cured Meat and Country Ham**, next.

> **Margin note:** cold smoke never cooks. The meat stays under about 30 C, so the smoke only flavours and guards the surface while the air does the drying, a few hours of smoke a day over weeks. That's why it belongs to the cool months, why the hours needn't come in a row, and why it keeps for months where hot smoked keeps for days.

> **Old names:** across the Balkans, beef, mutton and pork loin are salted, then hung in the *pušnica* over a smouldering fire through the winter: *suho meso* in Bosnia, *suva pečenica* in Serbia; Spain's *cecina de León* is beef smoked over oak the same way. Scotland and Wales salted and smoked a leg of mutton as a *mutton ham*, and Norway air-dries one as *fenalår*. On the American frontier a deer was cut in strips, salted and dried over a slow smoky fire: *jerked venison*, a word settlers learned from the Quechua *ch'arki* by way of Spanish, the same root as charque. On the Volga the same treatment makes *balyk* from sturgeon backs and *kholodnogo kopcheniya* fish.

## 11. Dry Cured Meat and Country Ham

Dry curing is the winter road for meat: a long salting in the cold, then weeks of hanging in cool shade, with smoke or without. It gives meat you eat as it is, sliced thin, for months, and from a leg of pork the **Country Ham**.

**The road, step by step**

1. **Salt it in the cold months.** **Pack Meat in Salt**, then **Country Cure Salted Meat** in the tub (chapter 5): **10 days** for cuts, **21** with a Leg of Pork. Only while the last ten days average **10 C or less**, about **October to April**: warmer, the long cure spoils in the tub.
2. **Hang it in the shade** with **Hang Salted Meat to Dry**: in the **Smokehouse**, on **any drying station under a roof**, or on a **Frame or Hanging Beam in a room or a basement**. Never in the open: sun and rain are for charque.
3. **Smoke it, or don't.** In a Smokehouse with a Firebox, bank the fire a few hours a day until the stack has its hours of smoke: **60** for cuts, **120** with a leg, on days of 15 C or less (chapter 10). Anywhere else, or with no fire, it air dries.
4. **Wait.** Three weeks for a cut and six for a leg at full speed; longer in the cold (below).
5. **Take it down** and eat it as it is. What it's called goes by its animal and its smoke:

| From | With its smoke | Air dried, no smoke |
|---|---|---|
| Beef, Steak | **Suho Meso** | **Bresaola** |
| Mutton Chop | **Mutton Ham** | **Dry Cured Mutton** |
| Venison | **Jerked Venison** | **Dry Cured Venison** |
| Pork, Pork Chop | **Smoked Dry Cured Pork** | **Dry Cured Pork** |
| Leg of Pork | **Smoked Country Ham** | **Country Ham** |

| | Dry cured cuts | Country Ham |
|---|---|---|
| Weight | about 50% of the raw cut | about 50% of the leg: 1.25 from a 2.5 leg |
| Food | 90% | 90% |
| Keeps | **120 / 180 days** (rinsed 108 / 162) | **180 / 365 days** |
| Eat it | **As it is**, sliced thin: a little salty, a small mood lift | **As it is**: a better mood lift |
| Aged after | **30 days** (22½ left hanging) | **90 days** (67½ left hanging) |
| Cook with it | Soups, stews, pies, rice, pasta, sandwiches, salads, tacos, burritos; **sliced**, as Ham Slices | **Carved** into slices, as Ham Slices |

**How fast it dries**

- **By the heaviest piece:** **3 weeks** for a Beef, Venison or Pork, a little less for a Steak or a chop, **6 weeks** for a Leg of Pork, at full speed.
- **Full speed is a cellar's cool:** from **12 C** up. Colder air dries slower, down to **a quarter** of the speed in frost, and never stops. Damp air slows it as it does salt fish. Wind plays no part.
- **A fire helps**, in the Smokehouse only: banked, the shed is 15 C warmer.

| Where a cut hangs | In frost | At 6 C | From 12 C | Smoke? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Smokehouse, fire out; any station under a roof; a Frame or Beam in a room | 12 weeks | 6 weeks | 3 weeks | no |
| Smokehouse, fire banked all day | 3 to 4 weeks | 3 weeks | 3 weeks | yes |

A Frame or Beam in a **basement** goes by the cellar's own temperature, which runs a month behind the season, at two thirds of the speed (still air):

| Hung in a basement in | A cut | A leg |
|---|---|---|
| December to February | 9 to 11 weeks | 17 to 19 weeks |
| March | 8 to 9 weeks | 14 to 15 weeks |
| April | 7 weeks | 12 weeks |
| May | 6 weeks | 10 weeks |
| June to October | 4½ to 5 weeks | 9 to 11 weeks |
| November | 6 to 7 weeks | 16 to 18 weeks |

> *A leg takes about twice as long as a cut. With a fire a few hours a day, a cut hung in the Smokehouse in December is done in about seven weeks and a leg in about three months; hung in March, a cut takes five weeks.*

- **The basement is the curing cellar:** the weather never reaches it, only the season, a month late. It is slow in midwinter (the cold that keeps the cure safe slows the drying too) and at its best from June to October, and in frost it still beats an unheated shed. The Gantry can't be built there.
- **The cellar is cool for the salt, too.** A Salting Tub in a basement goes by the cellar's temperature: fish and Corned meat never take a warm cure there, and a long cure loses far less than upstairs (a leg started in May about 4%, where upstairs it rots). It is still no summer cure: from June to September a leg in the cellar loses a third to two thirds of its keeping, and the cellar is still warm in October (about 18%). Salt from November to May.
- **If a stack loses its roof** it waits (*Stopped (Needs a roof)*).
- **No smoke outside the shed.** Suho Meso, Mutton Ham, Jerked Venison and the smoked hams need the Smokehouse and its Firebox.

**Mind the salt**

- **Use the long cure.** *Country Cured* meat has salt for the whole hang. **Corned** meat hasn't: hung in the Smokehouse, a room or a basement it dry cures too, but its popup shows a red **Under-salted** line and it spoils by the temperature where it hangs, a fire's warmth included. Near freezing it comes out about half way through its keeping; at 10 C it rots in four weeks, in a summer basement (13 C) in two and a half, and a banked fire makes the shed 15 C warmer than the day.
- **Corned meat has two good roads:** a hot cook in the Smokehouse (chapter 10), or the sun (chapter 8). Corned pork has only the first.
- **A rotted stack comes down as what you hung:** *Corned Beef (Rotten)*, not Bresaola. In a mixed stack only the Corned pieces rot.
- **You can see rot from across the yard** (0.4.21): a stack that is lost turns grey-green, mottled and mouldy on the Frame, Beam or Gantry, one look each for salt fish, cold dried fish and meat. A mixed stack whose Corned share rotted keeps its drying look while the Country Cured pieces dry on; once it is done, it shows rotten if any piece in it rotted. Sort it as you take it down.

**Ageing**

Dried meat is ready, but it isn't finished. Left alone it **ages** on its own food clock, as stockfish matures: a fridge slows it, a freezer stops it.

| | Aged after | Left hanging | Young | **Aged** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dry cured cut | **30 days** | **22½ days** | Unhappiness -3 | **Unhappiness -8** |
| Country Ham | **90 days** | **67½ days** | Unhappiness -10 | **Unhappiness -40, Boredom -20** |

- **The name tells you:** *Suho Meso*, then *Suho Meso (Aged)*; *Country Ham*, then *Country Ham (Aged)*. The tooltip counts the tenths on the way.
- **Leave it hanging and it ages in three quarters of the time.** A finished stack still on its Frame, Beam, Gantry or in the Smokehouse is hanging. **Once you take a piece down it can't go back up**, and it ages at the shelf's pace from then on.
- **Hanging meat holds its place.** A ham left to age in the Smokehouse keeps its stack for two more months. That is the price, and why the old farms had a meat house.
- **Young is fine.** Nothing is lost by eating it sooner but the mood. Hot Smoked meat, Ham and Charque never age.
- **The ham's reward is its mood, not its calories:** a leg makes the same food as the Pork it came from. A whole Aged ham is worth forty points of cheer and twenty of boredom, shared out bite by bite and slice by slice.

**The knife**

On any surface, with a sharp knife or cleaver, as the base game's Slice Ham:

| Recipe | Takes | Gives |
|---|---|---|
| **Slice Dry Cured Meat** | One dry cured cut | **2 to 4 slices** by its size (*Suho Meso Slice*, *Bresaola Slice (Aged)*) |
| **Slice Hot Smoked Meat** | One Hot Smoked cut | **2 to 6 slices** by its size (*Beef Slice (Hot Smoked)*) |
| **Carve Country Ham** | A Country Ham, **kept** | **5 slices**, and the ham back, lighter |
| **Carve the Last of the Country Ham** | A ham with one carving left | **5 slices**; the ham is gone |
| Slice Ham (the base game's) | A Ham from hot smoked pork | 6 Ham Slices |

| Slices from | Hot Smoked | Dry cured |
|---|---|---|
| Beef, Venison | 6 | 4 |
| Steak | 3 | 2 |
| Mutton Chop | 2 | 2 |
| Leg of Pork | - | 15, in three carvings |

- **A slice is a good mouthful: hunger 14 or so**, a little more than the game's Ham Slice (10). It goes where Ham Slices go: sandwiches, burgers, pizza, omelettes, salads, soups, stews, rice and pasta.
- **A slice takes its share** of the piece's food, its salt and its mood, and stays *Aged* if the piece was.
- **A cut face keeps half as long.** Dry cured slices **60 / 90 days**, Country Ham slices **90 / 180**, Hot Smoked slices **3 / 7**; a slice starts as far through its days as the piece was through its own.
- **So carve what you need.** A whole ham gives **three carvings, 15 slices**, and what stays on the bone keeps its own long clock and goes on ageing.

> *A ham hung in the Smokehouse in December, dried by March and left on its hook is Aged by late May. Taken down in March and put on a shelf, by mid June.*

> **Margin note:** why does the cure want the cold when the hang doesn't? Salt takes weeks to reach the middle of a joint, and until it does the meat by the bone is as raw as the day it was cut. That is why every farm killed its hogs after the first hard frost. Once the salt is through, a ham hangs happily into summer: Kentucky calls it the "summer sweat", and it is where the flavour comes from.

> **Old names:** the Italian *cantina* and the Alpine cellar are where *bresaola*, *coppa* and *lonza* hang: cool, still and a little damp, so the outside doesn't harden before the middle has dried. Bresaola is beef salted and hung in mountain air with no smoke at all, and Kentucky hung its country hams smoked or plain, as the family liked them. A country ham was sold young at three months and prized at a year, when the knife squeaked on it; Spain hangs its *jamón* on a stand and carves it a plateful at a time for the same reason this guide does: the cut face is the part that goes.

## 12. Lard, Tallow and Cracklings

**Rendering, step by step**

1. **Put Pork Fat in a Pot** (or **Put Suet in a Pot**): an empty Cooking Pot or Forged Pot and **2 pieces** of raw fat. Beef Suet and Mutton Fat share a pot, mixed or not.
2. **Cook it** on any stove, campfire or fireplace: about an hour. No station needed.
3. **Pour Off Lard** (or **Pour Off Tallow**): your pot now holds half a litre of **Lard (Melted)** (or **Tallow (Melted)**).
4. **Strain Lard** (or **Strain Tallow**) through a cloth or a Strainer: one block of fat and one portion of **Cracklings**. The pot and the cloth are kept.

| Strains fat | Notes |
|---|---|
| Cheese Cloth, Dish Towel, Rag, Sheet | Clean and dry |
| Strainer | The kitchen kind |

Melted fat is a liquid like any other: pour several pots into a bucket and strain it later. It does not spoil.

| Item | Keeps (fresh / rotten) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Pork Fat, Beef Suet, Mutton Fat | 2 / 4 days | Raw, it will make you sick. Fry a piece if you must |
| Lard | does not spoil | The base game's own Lard |
| Tallow | does not spoil | Bakes and seasons wherever Lard does |
| Cracklings | 7 / 14 days | A crisp snack; goes in salads, soups, stews and sandwiches |

> **Don't walk away.** Left on the heat for two and a half hours the fat burns. A burnt pot gives nothing: **Scrape Out Burnt Fat** gets you the pot back.

> **Margin note:** a pig on the hook gives 2 or 3 blocks of Lard, about as many calories as its meat. That is close to a real lard pig, and the reason the old breeds were kept.

**A whole pig at once: the Hearth's copper**

A pot takes two pieces. The copper of a **Hearth** (TP Core) takes the lot.

1. Right-click the Hearth: **Put Fat in the Copper**. Every piece of that kind you carry goes in. Pork fat on its own; suet and mutton fat together.
2. **Light the fire.** Once the copper steams (85 C), the fat needs **one hour** to melt. A banked fire is too cool.
3. **Fill from Copper** into a pot, a bucket or jars: you now carry **Lard (Melted)** or **Tallow (Melted)**.
4. **Strain** it: each half litre gives one block and one Cracklings.

| | Pot | Copper |
|---|---|---|
| Pieces at once | 2 | up to 240 |
| Time | 1 hour | heating up, then 1 hour steaming (a pig's fat: about 1 h 45 min) |
| Can it burn? | Yes, if left on the heat 2.5 hours | Never |
| Yield | 2 pieces → 1 block + Cracklings | the same |

Raw fat left in a cold copper goes **rancid** when its oldest piece would have rotted, and is only good for **Empty the Copper**. Melted fat keeps, in the copper or out of it.

> **Margin note:** the kettle in the yard on hog-killing day had a little water in the bottom, so the fat melted before it could scorch. That is why the copper never burns it. Straining is what makes lard keep: fat with scraps of meat left in it turns rancid in weeks.

> **Old names:** Balkan cooks call cracklings *čvarci* and make them every winter at the pig slaughter. In Britain, tallow lit the house and suet went into the pudding.

## 13. Potted Meat

Potted Meat is the road for any season and any red meat, pork too: cuts cooked slowly in Lard or Tallow with salt, then packed into a clay jar under their own fat. No station: a pot, a fire and a jar.

1. **Put Meat in Lard or Tallow** (right-click the meat or the fat): an empty **Pot** or Forged Pot, a block of **Lard** or **Tallow**, one cut and **1 Salt**. You get a **Pot of Meat in Fat**.
   Or, straight from rendering: **Put Meat in the Melted Fat** takes the pot of half a litre of **Lard (Melted)** or **Tallow (Melted)** that Pour Off leaves, one cut and Salt. Half a litre is one block.
2. **Add Meat to the Pot**, up to **four cuts** in all: a block of fat covers four. **Beef**, **Steak**, **Mutton Chop**, **Venison**, **Pork** and **Pork Chop** all go in.
3. Cook it on any stove or fire for about **two hours**. It burns if you leave it four, and then only **Scrape Out Burnt Fat** gets the pot back. Once it's cooked, no more meat goes in.
4. **Pack Potted Meat** into an empty **Clay Jar**, **Glazed Clay Jar** or **Clay Pot**: the pot comes back, and you have a **Crock of Potted Meat**, one portion for each cut.
5. Eat from it like any food: Eat Quarter, Half or All take portions, and the name counts down ("Crock of Potted Meat (3 portions)"). The **jar or pot comes back** with the last bite.

| | Pot of Meat in Fat | Crock of Potted Meat |
|---|---|---|
| Keeps | 2 / 4 days, as raw meat | **by place: see below** |
| Holds | 1 to 4 cuts | a portion a cut |
| Food | the cuts, plus a quarter block of fat for each | the same, cold and ready to eat |
| Eat it? | No: cook it, then pack it | Yes |

**A portion** is the cut's own food plus a quarter of the fat, about 1,000 calories of it. A full crock of four is around **5,000 calories**: few foods carry more. Fewer than four cuts leave the rest of the block behind in the pot, so cook four at a time when you can.

**Where you keep it.** The crock keeps by the temperature it sits in, and it remembers: one left a month in a basement has aged a basement's month when you next look.

| Place | Fresh | Rotten |
|---|---|---|
| Powered fridge, or 4 C and colder outdoors | **120 days** | 180 days |
| Basement (about 5 C from January to March, 13 C from July to September) | **110 to 64 days** | 165 to 96 days |
| A room, by the season | **120 to 14 days** | 180 to 21 days |
| A room while the grid is on (22 C) | **18 days** | 27 days |
| Powered freezer | stops | stops |

A pantry, kitchen or shed is unheated once the power's gone: it follows the average of the last ten days outdoors, on any floor. While the grid is on, a room is 22 C, as vanilla keeps it. A winter pantry keeps it like a fridge, a spring or autumn one about two months, a July one barely two weeks. **The first portion you take** lets air under the fat: a third of the time the crock has left goes at once, only that once. Open it when you mean to eat it out.

**The fat counts.** A Lard that has been used in cooking only gives what is left of it: a tenth of a block covers one cut with a tenth of the fat, and the next cut gets none.

> **Margin note:** keep it cold. Farmhouses kept their crocks in cellars through the winter, for months. Modern food safety is stricter: meat sealed under fat is a cold-storage food, kept below about 4 C for no more than three months, under at least a finger's width of fat. Warm, it is a botulism risk, whatever the seal looks like.

> **Old names:** Turkish *kavurma* and Central Asian *qovurma* are mutton or beef fried slowly in its own fat until the water is gone, packed into clay pots and sealed. French *confit* means "preserved"; the year's duck and goose were kept under their fat in glazed *pots à confit*. In the Balkans it is *meso u masti*, "meat in fat": pork in its own lard, put down after the autumn pig-killing.

## 16. Three Larders

Three ways to live, and what each should build. For a yardstick: a survivor needs something like **2,500 calories a day**; a Beef or a Venison is about 440, a Steak 220, a Pork 300, a chop 150 to 230, fresh fish about 400 for each 1.0 of weight, and a block of Lard or Tallow about 4,100.

| Cow or bull | 25 Beef, 25 Steaks | 16,500 | 6 to 7 | 200 |
| Pig | 19 Pork, 19 Pork Chops (one or two legs among them) | 8,500, and as much again in Lard | 3, or 7 with the Lard | about 150 |
| Sheep | 22 Mutton Chops | 5,000 | 2 | 88 |
| Deer | 26 Venison | 11,500 | 4 to 5 | 104 |

### The house with one freezer

*A town house, a generator, one fridge-freezer, no land.*

**The freezer is a waiting room, not a larder.** It holds only so much, it drinks fuel, and the day the generator stops you have about two days to save what's in it.

- **Freeze what's waiting for the tub.** A deer is 26 cuts and the tub cures them in two days, but the frame may be full. Frozen meat keeps while it waits. **Thaw it before you pack it**: the salt refuses frozen meat and fish.
- **Hot smoke once a week.** Corn a tub of meat or fish on Friday, stoke the Firebox on Sunday afternoon: a week or two of food that needs no power at all.
- **Keep crocks in the fridge.** A Crock of Potted Meat keeps **120 days** in a powered fridge and holds about 5,000 calories. Six crocks are twelve days of food on one shelf.
- **Dry the rest.** Summer: charque and salt fish on a frame under the porch roof. Winter: country cure, then a Hanging Beam in the basement.
- **Don't freeze stockfish or crocks you want to age.** A freezer stops stockfish maturing. Frozen fish is fine to *hang* for stockfish, though.

**The day the power dies**, work in this order:

| Order | What | Buys you |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Let it thaw. Cook and eat what you can today | 2 days |
| 2 | **Pack in salt and corn** everything else, a tub at a time | 7 / 14 days as Corned meat or Salt Cured fish |
| 3 | From the tub: **hot smoke** a batch, **pot** another under Lard | 1 to 2 weeks; weeks to months |
| 4 | Hang the rest: fish to salt dry, red meat to charque in summer | 4 to 6 months |

### The farm

*Cattle, pigs and sheep, a garden, a basement, a Hearth in the yard.*

The farm's year turns on two slaughters.

- **Summer beef, June to August.** A cow is 50 cuts: two tub loads and **200 salt**, corned in two days each. One Drying Frame in the sun takes the whole cow, five Beef to a stack: **charque** in one to two weeks, about 10 weight of it, good until December to February. The garden feeds you meanwhile.
- **Hog killing, November**, once the nights are frosty.
  1. **Render the fat** in the Hearth's copper the same day: two or three blocks of Lard.
  2. **Legs into the long cure first**, 21 days; cuts follow for 10. The tub holds one cure at a time, so a second tub earns its keep here.
  3. **Pot eight to twelve cuts** under the pig's own Lard: two or three crocks in the winter pantry, good until March.
  4. **Corn a few cuts and hot smoke them**: Ham for the week of the slaughter.
  5. **Mid-December: hang the Country Cured meat.** On a Hanging Beam in the basement the cuts are Dry Cured Pork by late February and the legs **Country Ham** by the end of April. In the Smokehouse with a fire each day they are quicker (seven weeks and three months) and come out smoked.
- **A winter steer or a few sheep, January to March**: country cure for 10 days, then the Smokehouse: **Suho Meso** and **Mutton Ham** for the spring.

### The river camp

*A hunter and fisher: no power, no fields, no cellar. A Salting Tub and a Drying Frame under a lean-to roof (Carpentry 2), and whatever the woods and the water give.*

## 17. The Logbook

*Found in a tackle box, the pages stiff with salt and smelling faintly of smoke.*

### Food you can carry

Drying takes the water out and leaves the food in. Here's what one unit of inventory weight is worth, made from a fish big enough to give guts and a head:

| Stage | Calories per 1.0 weight | Against fresh |
|---|---|---|
| Fresh fish | about 400 | × 1 |
| Salt Dried | about 975 | × 2½ |
| Stockfish | about 2,000 | × 5 |
| Soaked (cold) | about 670 | back to heavy |
| Fish Head | about 240 | it's mostly bone |

> *Margin note:* every vanilla fish carries the same calories for its weight. So a stack of stockfish weighing 2.5 holds as much food as about 12 weight of fresh fish. Rehydrate it at the pot, not on the road. Charque does the same for beef at nearly twice the raw calories per weight, and a crock of Potted Meat carries about 5,000.

### Why it works the way it does

- **Why does salt dry fish and meat?** Salt draws water out through the flesh, and bacteria can't live in what's left. That's why packed fish keeps only days, cured fish a week or two, and fully dried fish for months.
- **Why press it?** The board and stones squeeze out the brine the salt has pulled, so drying starts from a firmer, drier piece. Volga salters weight the fish in the vat under a wooden lid and stones, the *gnyot*; the charqueadas piled their salted beef and let the pile's own weight press it.
- **Why won't a room take gutted fish?** Stockfish dries in moving air at around freezing: cold enough that nothing rots, dry and windy enough that the water leaves. In a room the air goes still.
- **Why dry salt fish in the shade?** In full summer sun the skin dries hard before the inside does, trapping the wet, and the fat turns rusty. Volga salters hang their vobla under awnings and in open sheds.
- **Why does charque want the sun?** Lean beef has little fat to turn rancid, and heavy salt keeps flies and rot off while the heat drives the water out in days.
- **Why do warm stacks rot?** Unsalted fish has nothing protecting it but the cold. Once the season averages above 10 C, it spoils faster than it dries.
- **Why gut it first?** The guts spoil first and fastest. Stockfish makers gutted and headed every fish before it went on the rack.
- **Why does hot smoking need its hours in a row?** It's cooking. Heat that stops and starts warms the fish and lets it cool again; it never reaches the heat that cooks it through.
- **Why is cold smoke a winter job?** A smoulder lifts the shed about 15 C over the air. On a cold day that's a gentle, drying warmth; on a summer day it puts the meat at blood heat, where it spoils or half-cooks before it dries.

### Old names, old ways

- **On the lower Volga** around Astrakhan, fish is salted under the *gnyot*, soaked, and dried in the shade: small fish such as vobla whole with their heads on, large ones such as pike split or hung from hooks. That is this mod's salt route.
- **Stockfish** comes from the Lofoten islands of northern Norway, hung on wooden racks called *hjell* from February to May. It was traded across Europe from the Viking age on, and keeps for years.
- **Stockfish was never eaten off the rack.** After about three months on the *hjell*, it was stacked on pallets in dry, airy warehouses for two or three more months to mature, then sorted by hand into a dozen or more grades for Italy, Nigeria and the rest of the trade. It still travels in bales of 25 or 50 kg, wrapped in jute.
- **The heads weren't wasted.** Norway still dries its stockfish heads separately and ships them to West Africa, where they're prized for soups and stews.
- **Charque** fed the cattle country of southern Brazil and was shipped north to the plantations; in a good season the *charqueadas* by the rivers of Rio Grande do Sul salted and sun-dried cattle by the hundred thousand.
- **The Kentucky smokehouse** stood behind every farmhouse: hams and bacon salted after the first hard frost, hickory-smoked through the winter and hung there into summer.

## 18. Secrets & Hidden Features

- **Fish keep their identity.** A *Small Largemouth Bass* stays one through every stage. Each fish keeps its own weight and calories, so a batch of mixed sizes comes out mixed too. Meat keeps its cut in the tub (*Steak (Corned)*) and its animal after (*Beef Charque*, *Jerked Venison*).
- **Icons change with the cure.** Pale with salt when cured, browned when salt dried, grey when cold dried, gold when hot smoked, smoky bronze when cold smoked, darker again when rehydrated. Gutted fish are drawn **without their heads**, as stockfish were; halves show red flesh and a backbone. Meat goes from red to cured red-brown, charque nearly black, hot smoked mahogany, Dry Cured a deep burgundy.
- **Fish Guts are free chum.** Packing or gutting a whole fish over 0.6 weight gives Fish Guts weighing 10% of the fish.
- **Fillet at any stage.** A whole fish over 0.6 weight can still be cut into 2 fillets after it's been salted, dried or rehydrated, just like a fresh one. The fillets stay at the same stage.
- **Changed your mind?** A Gutted Fish or Fish Half can still be packed in salt. It won't give a second lot of guts.
- **Damp air matters a little.** Air more humid than 50% slows salt drying and charque, though the game's air is rarely that damp.
- **Every frame looks different.** Each stack picks its hanging look at random (whole, headless or split), so a full frame looks hand-hung. Charque hangs as dark cuts, red going to deep brown.
- **The ham by the door.** A Smokehouse with finished stacks inside hangs a smoked ham by its door, so you can see from across the yard that something's ready.
- **Salt Dried fish needs no soaking for soup.** Toss it straight into a pot. Stockfish and charque do: they're too hard to eat as they are.
- **Dishes show one steady icon** for each preserved ingredient, so a stew of mixed dried fish doesn't flicker.

## 19. Tips from the Docks and the Block

- **Fillets don't cheat the salt.** A vanilla fillet weighs 5% of its fish but holds half its food, so salted it pays and weighs as half the gutted fish.
- **Big catch?** A fish heavier than a stack's limit won't fit. Split it in two, or hang it on the Beam or Gantry.
- **An open shed beats a room.** A frame under a porch or lean-to roof dries salt fish and charque at full speed, rain or shine, in the shade.
- **Winter is stockfish season.** Gut everything you catch in December and January. Turn the heads into stock the same day, as they spoil like fresh fish.
- **Don't eat December's stockfish in January.** Give it the 60 days. A crate in the shed is enough, a pallet in the loft is better, and the freezer is the one place it won't mature.
- **Kill hogs after the first frosts.** The long cure is only safe from October to April. Salt the legs first: they take 21 days in the tub and the tub holds one cure at a time.
- **A cellar beam is a ham's best friend.** A Hanging Beam in a basement dries Country Cured meat with no fire to tend and never sees rain or frost: about a month a cut from June to October, two to three in the depth of winter.
- **Butcher on the hook.** The Butchering Hook gives nearly five times the fat of the ground.
- **Cook four cuts to a crock**, and keep it in the basement or a winter pantry. Open it when you mean to finish it.
- **Hot smoke what you'll eat this week**, and salt-dry the rest. A small batch of fish cooks in four hours of fire.
- **In winter, bank the Smokehouse a few hours a day.** A banked Firebox burns a third of the wood; fish has its smoke in under a week and meat in a week or two, and both dry faster for the warmth.
- **Rehydrate only what you'll cook today.** Soaked fish and soaked charque keep 2 days.
- With the **Salt mod**, a salt lick keeps you packing forever. See the Collection Guide.

| Wanted Cold Smoked, got Salt Dried; wanted Suho Meso, got Bresaola | The stack was short of its hours of smoke (the popup's *Smoke* line), or the days were over 15 C (*Too warm to count*). Bank the Firebox more of the time, in a cooler month |
| Meat in the shed says *Under-salted* and spoils | It is Corned. Give it its hot cook, or use Country Cure Salted Meat for meat that hangs |
| A stack came down as *Corned Beef (Rotten)* or a rotten pressed fish | It rotted before it was done, so it is still what you hung |
| A finished stack looks rotten but some pieces are fine | It shows rotten when any piece rotted: a mixed Corned and Country Cured stack. Take it down and sort it |
| Meat in the shed takes months | Dry curing is slow in frost. A banked fire a few hours a day warms the shed; March and April are the quick months |
| Wanted Charque, got Suho Meso or Bresaola | Meat hung in the Smokehouse is always dry cured. For charque, use a frame in the sun |
| Drying Rate didn't rise with the fire | Below about -2 C outdoors a banked fire can't lift salt fish off its slowest rate (27 days); stoke it, which helps from about -7 C. Over about 5 C banked is already at full speed, so stoking adds nothing to drying |
| Add Meat to the Pot is greyed out | The pot already has four cuts, or it has cooked: meat goes in raw |
| Pack Potted Meat is greyed out | The pot isn't cooked yet, or it burnt (Scrape Out Burnt Fat). The jar must be empty |
| Put Meat in the Melted Fat is missing | The pot needs a full half litre of Lard (Melted) or Tallow, as Pour Off leaves it |

## 22. Appendix: The Tester's Toolkit

*For testers, modders and the curious. These commands exist only when the game is started in debug mode (Steam launch option `-debug`). Type them into the in-game **Command Console** and press Enter. `TPFM.help()` lists them in the game.*

> **Warning:** They change your save directly, so try them on a test save, not a playthrough you care about.

**Weather and time**

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `TPFM.weather()` | The drying weather now: temperature, the 10-day season average, the basement's temperature and its drying and tub rates, humidity, wetness, the wind, the drying speeds, whether unsalted and salted fish are spoiling, and the running totals |
| `TPFM.here()` | Which place you're standing in, as a station there would read it: Open, Covered (a roof, no room), Sheltered (a room) or Basement. |
| `TPFM.wind()` | Starts a log of the live wind every 10 minutes; run it again to print it beside the forecast |
| `TPFM.age(hours)` | Gives every hanging stack this many hours of perfect drying weather. `TPFM.age(288)` finishes a salt stack, `TPFM.age(768)` a cold one, `TPFM.age(120)` a charque one |
| `TPFM.rain(hours)` | Adds hours of rain (default 6) and wets the open stations: an open charque stack loses a tenth per 6 hours. Then `TPFM.dry()` |
| `TPFM.dry()` | Clears the wetness, so open and cold stations start drying again at once |
| `TPFM.spoil(hours)` | Adds spoiling weather, to test rotting. A fresh cold stack rots after 48 |
| `TPFM.frost(hours)` | Adds hours of hard frost (72 hours take a tenth of a fresh cold stack's keeping) |
| `TPFM.heat(hours)` | Adds hours of hot sun (24 hours take a twentieth of a fresh open salt stack's keeping) |
| `TPFM.warmCure(hours)` | Adds hours of a warm cure at 25 C, to test a warm cure spoiling a running tub (24 hours take about a fifth) |
| `TPFM.mature(days)` | Ages the stockfish and the dry cured meat you carry by this many days. Stockfish is Matured after 60, a cut Aged after 30, a Country Ham after 90 |
| `TPFM.food()` | The cured fish and meat you carry, with the numbers the game rounds or hides: hunger, unhappiness, boredom, thirst, weight, age against its days, ageing |

**Spawning**

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `TPFM.fish(name, n, weight, stage)` | Spawns `n` fish, at that weight if given (food scaled to match), e.g. `TPFM.fish("Walleye", 2, 4)`. A stage gives it further along: `"cured"` (Salt Cured, ready to hang), `"dried"` (Salt Dried), `"hotSmoked"`, `"coldSmoked"` (each as its station gives a cured fish, nothing spoiled) or `"stockfish"` (young, fresh off the frame), and may take the weight's place: `TPFM.fish("Walleye", 3, "cured")`. `TPFM.fish()` lists the names; `"all"` gives one of each |
| `TPFM.meat(name, n, stage)` | Spawns `n` of a cut, e.g. `TPFM.meat("Steak", 2)`; `"all"` gives each of Beef, Steak, Mutton Chop, Venison, Pork, Pork Chop and Leg of Pork. A stage gives it further along: `"corned"` (*Steak (Corned)*; no leg), `"country"` (Country Cured), `"charque"` (a Corned cut dried on a frame; no pork), `"hotSmoked"` (a Corned cut from a stoked Smokehouse; pork comes out as the game's Ham, no leg) or `"dryCured"` (dry cured, smoked and young, to age and slice). `TPFM.meat()` lists the names |
| `TPFM.give(name, n, option)` | Spawns `n` of: `"head"` (Fish Head), `"stock"` (Pot of Fish Head Stock), `"salt"` (vanilla shaker), `"fat"` (one each of Pork Fat, Beef Suet and Mutton Fat), `"fatPot"` (a Pot of Pork Fat, cooked; option `"burnt"` or `"raw"`), `"crock"` (a full Crock of Potted Meat: four Steaks in Lard in a Glazed Clay Jar). Default food values. `TPFM.give()` lists them |
| `TPFM.kit("potted")` | Spawns what Potted Meat takes: a Pot, Lard, Salt, four cuts and the three clay vessels |
| `TPFM.kit("flue")` | Spawns both flue kits (2 Clay Pipe Segments, 3 Flat Stones, 3 Clay), a Mason's Trowel and a Shovel. With Core's `TPC.kit("firebox")` you can build and join a Firebox |

**Looking things over**

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `TPFM.pallet()` | The Stockfish Pallet near you: its place, ripening speed, stack and fish |
| `TPFM.fatRows()` | The fat each vanilla animal's butchering table gives now |
| `TPFM.crocks()` | Each Crock of Potted Meat on you or within 4 tiles: where it is, how fast it keeps there, days until stale and rotten |
| `TPFM.keep(days)` | Ages those crocks by this many days at their place |
| `TPFM.smokehouse()` | Each Smokehouse tile within 3 tiles: its facing, back or door tile, and each Firebox beside it with its flue and why it can't join (or "may join", "joined here") |
| `TPFM.smoke()` | The joined Firebox's fire record (stoked, banked, the run now going) and each stack's heaviest piece, hot hours and smoke against Cold Smoked's need |
| `TPFM.stoke(hours)` | Adds stoked hours to the run now going in those joined Fireboxes (stoke it first) |
| `TPFM.bank(hours)` | Adds banked hours (default 6) to those joined Fireboxes: smoke for Cold Smoked, if today is 15 C or less |

The Salting Tub runs on the game clock, not the weather, so `TPFM.age` doesn't speed up curing. Use the game's own time controls for that.

**Typical checks**
- **Salt fish:** `TPFM.fish("Walleye", 3, 4)` and `TPFM.give("salt", 10)` (each 4 weight walleye takes 28 salt), pack and cure, then hang the fish and `TPFM.age(288)`.
- **Charque:** `TPFM.meat("all", 2, "corned")`, hang the beef, mutton and venison on a frame (it refuses the pork), `TPFM.age(120)`, take them down and rehydrate one.
- **Potted Meat:** `TPFM.kit("potted")`, Put Meat in Lard or Tallow with the Steak, Add Meat to the Pot three times, cook it two hours, Pack Potted Meat into the Glazed Clay Jar, then eat a quarter.
- **Hot smoking:** build a Smokehouse, `TPC.kit("firebox")` and `TPFM.kit("flue")`, build a Firebox by a long side with its door turned away, Connect the Firebox, hang `TPFM.meat("all", 2, "corned")` inside, light the Firebox, then `TPFM.stoke(8)`: Hot Smoked meat, and a ham by the door.
- **Cold smoking:** in a cool month, the same Smokehouse and Firebox, hang `TPFM.fish("Walleye", 3, "cured")`, bank the Firebox, `TPFM.bank(50)`, then `TPFM.age(288)`: Cold Smoked Fish.
- **Dry curing:** hang `TPFM.meat("all", 1, "country")` (Country Cured, with a leg) in the Smokehouse. `TPFM.age(1008)` with no fire: Bresaola, the Dry Cured meats and a Country Ham. With `TPFM.addSmoke(130)` first (or `TPFM.bank(130)` on a day of 15 C or less): Suho Meso, Mutton Ham, Jerked Venison, Smoked Dry Cured Pork and a Smoked Country Ham. Hang `TPFM.meat("all", 1, "corned")` (Corned) and `TPFM.age(520)`: it comes down Corned and rotten, as it would after three weeks in a 12 C shed (`TPFM.underSalt(hours)` adds the spoiling alone). `TPFM.smoke()` lists each stack's hours.
- **Heads:** `TPFM.fish("Walleye", 1, 4)` and `TPFM.fish("Bluegill", 1, 0.9)`, then Gut Fish on both. Only the walleye gives a head.
